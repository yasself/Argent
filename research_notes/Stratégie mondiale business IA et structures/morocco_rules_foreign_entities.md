## 1. Exchange control: can a resident individual create/hold a foreign company, open foreign accounts, hold wallet balances? (IGOC 2026)

### Takeaway
Under the IGOC 2026, a Moroccan resident individual has **no general permission** to create or acquire shares in a foreign company, to open an account abroad, or to constitute any asset abroad: these operations are expressly listed as "subject to the authorisation of the Office des Changes", and the only individual outbound-investment cases the IGOC itself allows are employee share plans of a foreign parent and directors' qualifying shares. The price paid (even zero, or a few hundred dollars on the e-commerce card) is irrelevant: the definition of "avoirs à l'étranger" covers "any property, right and interest, whether or not represented by securities", which captures LLC membership interests and, by its wording ("liquidités en devises"), foreign bank/EMI/wallet balances.

### Cited Findings
- **General rule (art. 1 [derived], "Champ d'application")**: "Toute opération de change non expressément définie ou dont les modalités ou les conditions de réalisation ne correspondent pas à celles prévues par les dispositions de la présente Instruction, demeure soumise à l'autorisation de l'Office des Changes. Il s'agit notamment des opérations ci-après : … L'ouverture de comptes à l'étranger par les personnes marocaines résidentes ; La constitution d'avoirs à l'étranger par les personnes marocaines résidentes ; … Les règlements par voie de compensation ; Les avances de fonds ou facilités financières accordées par un résident à un non-résident". — [IGOC 2026, Chap. I, §1](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Definition of "avoirs à l'étranger" (art. 2 [derived])**: "les instruments financiers, les biens immeubles, les liquidités en devises, les avoirs en or et de façon générale, tout bien, droit et intérêt représenté ou non par des titres, détenus en dehors du territoire assujetti." — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Definition of "opérations en capital"**: "opérations portant sur des investissements, des placements, des financements et, de manière générale, sur des acquisitions, constitutions ou cessions d'avoirs ou prise d'engagements, sous quelque forme que ce soit." **"Opérations de change"** include "toute opération qui donne naissance à une dette ou à une créance entre résidents et non-résidents". — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Resident = tax resident**: "Résident : Personne physique marocaine ou étrangère considérée comme résidente au sens de la législation fiscale en vigueur ; Personne morale marocaine". "Non-résident : Personne physique considérée comme non-résidente au sens de la législation fiscale en vigueur ; Personne morale étrangère ; … Entité installée dans les places financières offshore et dans les zones d'accélération industrielle sises au Maroc." — [IGOC 2026, Définitions](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Outbound investment by individuals (art. 185 [x-ref])** is limited to two cases: "les participations des salariés actifs résidents de sociétés marocaines au capital des personnes morales étrangères détenant, directement ou indirectement, … au moins 51% dans le capital desdites sociétés marocaines" (capped at 10% of prior-year net salary, except free shares / cashless stock options) and "les actions de garantie" held by residents appointed directors/supervisory-board members of foreign companies in the framework of a Moroccan company's art.-178 investment. No other individual case exists in the chapter "Investissements marocains à l'étranger". — [IGOC 2026, Chap. V §2.2](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Declaration duty (art. 18 [derived], "Déclaration des avoirs et liquidités détenus à l'étranger")**: "Sauf dispositions règlementaires particulières, les personnes physiques marocaines résidentes qui entrent en possession d'avoirs ou de liquidités à l'étranger sont soumises à l'obligation de déclaration à l'Office des Changes de ces avoirs et liquidités dans un délai de trois (3) mois à compter de la date d'entrée desdits avoirs et liquidités dans leurs patrimoines … selon le modèle joint en annexe 3." — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **E-commerce card for individuals**: 20,000 MAD per calendar year per resident individual (art. 121 [derived]); the "commerce électronique" regime is defined as "tout achat en ligne réglé par carte de paiement internationale" (art. 120 [x-ref]) — i.e., a purchase/current-operation allowance, not an investment channel. — [IGOC 2026, Chap. IV §7](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Foreign-currency account for resident individuals with foreign-source income (art. 234 [x-ref])**: banks may open "comptes en devises ou en dirhams convertibles au nom des personnes physiques marocaines résidentes, non inscrites au registre de commerce, disposant de revenus de source étrangère" (income self-declared in writing to the bank with the nature of the operation). Credit: "Jusqu'à 70% des revenus de sources étrangères rapatriées. Le reliquat doit être cédé sur le marché des changes". Debit: "Tout règlement à destination de l'étranger, dans le cadre d'opérations courantes **à l'exclusion de toute acquisition de biens immeubles, d'actifs financiers ou toute autre constitution d'avoirs à l'étranger sous quelque forme que ce soit**"; and any dirham payment in Morocco. — [IGOC 2026, Chap. VI §3](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **OC public position on platforms**: in a MAP interview (17 Jan 2022) the OC Director General listed, among infractions found when controlling resident individuals earning foreign currency online, "le non rapatriement du produit de ces exportations de services", holding assets abroad "sans autorisation de l'Office des Changes" (company creation, real estate, financial assets, bitcoin/virtual currencies) and "l'ouverture de comptes bancaires à l'étranger que ce soit auprès d'organismes financiers réglementés ou auprès de plateformes financières (paypal, payoneer,…)", plus using export proceeds to pay foreign expenses by compensation. — [Finances News Hebdo, 17 Jan 2022](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/office-des-changes-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours)
- Accounting/advisory firms summarise the same rule: an operation not provided for by the IGOC requires prior OC authorisation; a resident individual opening an account abroad needs prior authorisation save exceptions. — [expertisecomptable.ma](https://www.expertisecomptable.ma/blog/principes-reglementation-des-changes-maroc/); [Cabinet AOS](https://cabinet-aos.com/blog/fiscalite/reglementations-changes-maroc/); contradicted by LLC-formation vendors' marketing claiming "100% légal" without citing any OC text — [creer-llc-usa.com](https://creer-llc-usa.com/articles/fr/llc-usa-maroc-2026), [yourllc.net](https://www.yourllc.net/blog/creer-llc-americaine-depuis-maroc-guide-ultime) (commercial sources, low reliability).

### Inferences
- A US LLC / UK Ltd / Estonian OÜ / UAE free-zone company formed online by a resident individual is a "constitution d'avoirs à l'étranger" and an "opération en capital" not provided for by the IGOC → **requires prior OC authorisation; without it, it is an exchange offence irrespective of the amount paid**. The LLC's Mercury/Wise/Stripe balance is additionally (i) an asset of an entity the individual holds without authorisation and (ii) if in the individual's own name (Wise personal, Payoneer, PayPal balance kept abroad) an "account opened abroad"/"liquidités en devises" held abroad. The OC's 2022 statement names PayPal and Payoneer explicitly.
- Receiving payments *through* a platform and promptly repatriating to a Moroccan bank account is different from *holding* a balance abroad; the IGOC obligation is repatriation of 100% of service-export proceeds within 90 days (see §4). Whether a transient PayPal/Payoneer balance pending withdrawal is tolerated is practice, not text — VALIDATE.
- The individual "compte en devises" (art. 234) is the only text-based way for an unregistered individual to keep foreign currency (70%) — but it is held **in a Moroccan bank** and cannot fund any foreign asset.

### Gaps
- No public OC text or FAQ was found stating whether authorisations for a resident *individual* to hold a foreign operating company are ever granted, nor any statistics; press and firm sources describe individual authorisations as case-by-case (e.g. [Cabinet PFC](https://cabinetpfc.com/demandes-dautorisation-de-loffice-des-changes/) offers assistance with OC authorisation requests; page not read in detail). Application channel is reported to be the OC's online platform ("SMART") — snippet only, VALIDATE.
- The IGOC does not mention EMI/wallets by name; treatment of wallet balances as "comptes à l'étranger" rests on the general definitions plus the OC's 2022 statement.
- Annex 3 (declaration form) content not reviewed.

## 2. "Investissements marocains à l'étranger" by legal persons, the ADD start-up derogation, and whether a small new SARL can hold a foreign subsidiary

### Takeaway
In the IGOC 2026 a Moroccan **company** may invest abroad freely (through its bank, no prior OC authorisation) up to **200 million MAD per year** — the 2026 text shows a single ceiling, no longer a per-continent split — but only if it has **≥3 years of activity**, **accounts certified without reservation by a statutory auditor**, and the investment is **related to its own activity** (no placements, no real estate); holdings on either side need prior authorisation. The new 2026 derogation lets an **ADD-labelled "jeune entreprise innovante" (JEI)** invest abroad up to **10 million MAD/year without the 3-year and audit conditions**, and lets founders of a JEI with a firm foreign-funding commitment "flip" into a foreign entity. A freshly created ordinary SARL does not qualify without either the JEI label or a specific OC authorisation.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition (art. 178 [x-ref])**: investments abroad "par les personnes morales marocaines inscrites au registre de commerce dans le but de consolider et de développer leurs activités et d'établir un intérêt économique durable" (excluding investments in Moroccan ZAI/offshore places). — [IGOC 2026, Chap. V §2.1](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Conditions (art. 179 [x-ref])**: "La personne morale … doit avoir au moins trois (3) années d'activité ; La comptabilité … doit être certifiée sans réserve par un commissaire aux comptes ; L'investissement … doit être en rapport avec l'activité de la personne morale résidente concernée, avoir pour objectif de consolider et de développer cette activité et ne pas porter sur des opérations de placements ou sur des biens immobiliers." — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Forms (art. 180 [x-ref])**: creation of companies; acquisition of ≥10% of a non-resident entity; liaison/representative offices or branches. Financing may be capital, shareholder loans/current-account advances (contract required, market-rate remuneration), equipment and operating-cost funding for offices/branches. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Ceiling**: "Le montant autorisé, par personne morale résidente et par année civile … peut atteindre deux cent (200.000.000) millions de dirhams", transfers "au fur et à mesure de l'avancement des projets et en fonction du besoin réel des entités créées à l'étranger." Banks may also transfer "les frais afférents à la constitution de sociétés". — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Still subject to prior OC authorisation**: investments abroad by resident **holdings**; investments creating a **holding abroad** or taking a stake in a foreign holding; operations absorbing cumulated losses; capital reductions of foreign subsidiaries; direct/indirect acquisition of foreign real estate; and outbound investments whose object is to invest (round-trip) in Morocco incl. ZAIs. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Documents to the bank (first transfer)**: Annex 9 information sheet, business plan, operating budget (offices/branches), auditor's attestation certifying the accounts "sans réserve" for the preceding financial year (the attestation is required again for each later transfer). — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Repatriation**: "Les revenus et produits de cession ou de liquidation d'investissements marocains à l'étranger doivent être rapatriés et cédés sur le marché des changes par les investisseurs dans un délai de trente (30) jours à compter de la date de leur mise en paiement"; reinvestment abroad of sale/liquidation proceeds allowed under arts. 179-181; shareholder loans must be repatriated within 5 years (or capitalised). Income to repatriate includes "les dividendes ou parts de bénéfices; les bénéfices réalisés par les succursales à l'étranger…; les intérêts". — [IGOC 2026, Chap. V §2.1.3 and Chap. IV §11.2](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **JEI derogation (art. 184 [derived], "Dispositions dérogatoires")**: (a) "Pour les jeunes entreprises innovantes en nouvelles technologies, labellisées par l'ADD, disposant d'un engagement ferme de financement auprès de bailleurs de fonds étrangers, leurs fondateurs sont autorisés à créer des entités de droit étranger en y apportant une partie ou la totalité de leurs parts dans lesdites jeunes entreprises"; investors (legal persons, individuals, OPCC, financial institutions) may likewise contribute their shares. (b) "Par dérogation aux dispositions de l'article 179 …, la JEI … est autorisée à effectuer des opérations d'investissements à l'étranger en rapport avec son activité sans justification des trois (3) années d'activité et de la certification par un commissaire aux comptes et ce, dans la limite de dix millions (10.000.000) de dirhams par année civile sur présentation à la banque d'une copie de l'attestation de labellisation délivrée par l'ADD et de la fiche … annexe 9". Income and sale proceeds: repatriate within 30 days. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- IGOC 2026 preamble confirms this is new in 2026: "Possibilité pour les start-ups labellisées par l'ADD de réaliser des investissements à l'étranger en rapport avec leur activité, à hauteur de dix millions (10.000.000) de dirhams par année civile… dispensées de la justification des trois (3) années d'activité et de la certification des comptes". — [IGOC 2026, Préambule](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **JEI e-commerce card**: 2,000,000 MAD per calendar year (raised from 1,000,000) for "services fournis en leur faveur en lien direct avec leurs activités", on presentation of the ADD attestation. — [IGOC 2026, Chap. IV §7](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- Press framing: before 2026 the seniority, audit and rigid-ceiling requirements blocked start-ups from creating foreign subsidiaries; the 2026 instruction is presented as "une avancée pour les startups". — [Médias24, 21 Jan 2026 (snippet only; page 403)](https://medias24.com/2026/01/21/la-nouvelle-instruction-de-change-apporte-une-avancee-pour-les-startups-1612357/); OC presented the 2026 changes at the CGEM on 19 Feb 2026 — [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/02/19/loffice-des-changes-presente-les-nouveautes-de-linstruction-generale-2026-a-la-cgem-1630913/)
- The JEI label is granted by the Agence de Développement du Digital by an "attestation de labellisation"; eligibility criteria are published on the ADD site; a 2021 controversy concerned how the label and its international card were granted. — [Médias24, 8 Feb 2021](https://medias24.com/2021/02/08/polemique-autour-de-loctroi-du-label-jei-et-sa-carte-de-paiement-internationale/); [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-entreprises/jeunes-entreprises-innovantes-un-dispositif-qui-fait-la-difference)

### Inferences
- **Legal route (ii)**: individual → Moroccan SARL/SARL AU (operating company, not a pure holding) → after 3 financial years + a statutory auditor certifying the accounts without reservation → 100% foreign subsidiary (US C-Corp/LLC, UK Ltd, OÜ, UAE FZ) whose activity is related to the Moroccan company's activity. A SARL below the legal thresholds has no mandatory auditor, so one must be appointed voluntarily to satisfy art. 179. Dividends of the foreign subsidiary must come back to Morocco within 30 days of being made payable; the subsidiary may retain (not distribute) profits abroad — the IGOC obliges repatriation of income "mise en paiement", it does not impose a distribution policy (VALIDATE with OC, and beware the "besoin réel" language on outbound funding).
- **Legal route (iii)**: individual → Moroccan company that obtains the ADD JEI label → foreign subsidiary immediately, up to 10 M MAD/year, no 3-year/audit requirement. This is the fastest text-based route to a foreign operating/payment entity for an AI/SaaS business. The "flip" (founders contributing their JEI shares to a foreign entity) additionally requires "un engagement ferme de financement auprès de bailleurs de fonds étrangers" (e.g., a signed VC term commitment) — it is not available just to hold a foreign shell.
- A foreign *pure holding* owned by the Moroccan company, or a Moroccan *holding* as investor, falls back under prior authorisation.
- The per-continent ceilings mentioned in the brief (e.g., 100 M Africa / 50 M elsewhere, later raised) correspond to earlier IGOC versions; the 2026 text read here states one figure, 200 M MAD per legal person per year.

### Gaps
- Current ADD JEI eligibility criteria (age of company, revenue, R&D, shareholder structure) were not retrieved from add.gov.ma; must be read on the ADD site — VALIDATE.
- Whether the JEI 10 M MAD derogation still requires the "en rapport avec son activité" test to be evidenced beyond Annex 9, and how banks apply it in practice in 2026: no source found.
- Whether a foreign subsidiary that is a US LLC taxed as disregarded/partnership raises issues for the bank: no source.

## 3. Sanctions, limitation, amnesties (2014 / 2020 / 2024) and what they revealed

### Takeaway
Exchange offences are punished under the dahir of 30 August 1949: press and official presentations consistently cite fines up to **six times the amount of the offence** and imprisonment (the dahir text is reported as one month to five years), with a transaction (settlement) power for the administration; press reports state there is **no limitation period**. Three "régularisation spontanée" windows (2014, 2020, 2024) show the State's doctrine: unauthorised foreign assets are illegal but regularisable against a contribution, with declared volumes falling from ~28 bn MAD (2014) to 5.93 bn (2020) to just over 2 bn (2024; 658 declarations).

### Cited Findings
- Fines can reach six times the value of the offence, with prison terms cited as three months to five years by press; the dahir text hosted by the Ministry of Industry is reported as "un mois à cinq ans"; a 2014 Ministry of Finance presentation speaks of a *minimum* of six times the corpus delicti (sources diverge on "up to" vs "minimum"); the administration may settle ("transiger") with the offender. — [Dahir du 30 août 1949, mcinet.gov.ma PDF (identified via search; not read line by line)](https://www.mcinet.gov.ma/sites/default/files/dahir%20du%2030-8-1949.pdf); [Ministry of Finance 2014 press-conference deck](https://www.finances.gov.ma/Publication/daag/2014/contr_lib_conf_presse.pdf); [Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/maroc-office-changes-fait-chasse-fraudeurs,109102.html)
- "Aucune prescription n'est prévue en matière d'infractions à la réglementation des changes"; 2024 control results: 2,469 files, transactions of 53.4 bn MAD, 206 infractions representing 4.28 bn MAD, 361 on-site investigations; main irregularities: holding assets abroad, non-repatriation of export proceeds and of foreign-investment income, undue import transfers; fine distribution: just over half under 100,000 MAD, 20% between 100,000 and 500,000, 9% between 500,000 and 1 M, 20% above 1 M MAD. — [Le360 (press review of Les Inspirations Éco), 11 Nov 2025](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/controles-renforces-et-sanctions-accrues-loffice-des-changes-passe-a-la-vitesse-superieure_BPWMHBBW7JCP5JIFC3WK2SBOKY/)
- 2025 (OC management report published July 2026): 2,521 control files covering > 72.3 bn MAD; 172 files with breaches referred to litigation; individuals a material share of controls (15.2% per TelQuel vs 21% per Hespress — unreconciled); unauthorised holding of assets abroad by individuals a main focus; 104 judicial requisitions of which 46 concerning individuals (26 the year before); 500 on-site investigations (+38%). — [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/482816-controle-sur-pieces-plus-de-723-mmdh-de-transactions-controlees-par-loffice-des-changes-en-2025.html); [TelQuel, 27 Jul 2026 (snippet only)](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2026/07/27/les-controles-de-loffice-des-changes-engrange-723-milliards-de-dh-en-2025_2000870/); [OC – publication of 2025 report](https://www.oc.gov.ma/en/actualites/publication-du-rapport-de-gestion-de-loffice-des-changes-de-lannee-2025)
- 129 contentious files in 2024. — [TelQuel, 4 Nov 2025](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2025/11/04/office-des-changes-129-dossiers-contentieux-en-2024_1959206/)
- **2024 amnesty** (art. 8 of LF 55-23 for 2024; closed 31 Dec 2024): 658 declarations, > 2 bn MAD; liquid assets only ~12% (244.7 M MAD). — [OC communiqué](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/actualites/communique-bilan-de-l-operation-de-regularisation-spontanee-au-titre-des-avoirs-et); [TelQuel, 7 Jan 2025](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2025/01/07/avoirs-a-letranger-658-declarations-en-2024-pour-plus-de-2-mmdh-selon-loffice-des-changes_1912028/); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/officiel-amnistie-sur-les-avoirs-detenus-a-letranger-un-total-de-658-declarations-pour-plus-de-2_MAWVHJLKGJGBJEETIGPXOTRUFU/)
- 2024 contribution: 10% of acquisition value of foreign real estate for first-time declarants (15% for those who had already used a previous amnesty, per Le Matin); anonymity guaranteed; no administrative or judicial proceedings on regularised assets after payment. — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/avoirs-detenus-a-letranger-ce-que-prevoit-le-budget-2024/206479); [Challenge](https://www.challenge.ma/fiscalite-contribution-liberatoire-la-derniere-chance-276563/)
- **2020 amnesty** (art. 8 LF 70-19): 5.93 bn MAD, 1,959 declarations. **2014** (art. 4 ter LF 110-13): ~28 bn MAD, contribution 2%–10%. — [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/amnistie-sur-les-avoirs-en-devises-pres-de-6-milliards-de-dirhams-declares-231014/); [Ministry of Finance 2014 deck](https://www.finances.gov.ma/Publication/daag/2014/contr_lib_conf_presse.pdf)
- The IGOC 2026 gives lasting rights to those who regularised: "les Marocains résidents détenant des comptes en devises ou en dirhams convertibles dans le cadre des dispositions de la loi 63-14, de l'article 4 Ter de la loi de finances n°110-13 …2014, de l'article 8 de la loi de finances n°70-19 …2020 et de l'article 8 de la loi de finances n°55-23 …2024" may use those accounts for e-commerce up to their balance and place the balances. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Three amnesties in ten years, each expressly covering assets held abroad "en infraction", confirm the official reading that residents' unauthorised foreign holdings (including company shares — the OC's 2022 list includes "création de sociétés") are offences, not a grey zone. No fourth window was found in force for 2025-2026; relying on a future amnesty is not a compliance strategy.
- With no limitation period reported and fines indexed on the amount, an unauthorised LLC that has collected revenue for years creates an exposure that grows with turnover.

### Gaps
- Exact article wording of the 1949 dahir (fine multiple, prison range, recidivism) not read directly — VALIDATE on the official text. Imprisonment in pure "freelancer" cases: no reported case found.
- Detailed 2024 rates for liquid/financial assets (5%? 2% for repatriated cash ceded?) not confirmed (the "5%" seen in the search related to the parallel domestic tax regularisation) — treat as unverified.
- Whether the 2024 law allowed declarants to *keep* foreign company shares after paying: not verified.

## 4. Architecture A mechanics under exchange control: exporting services from Morocco, 90-day repatriation, 70% foreign-currency retention, paying foreign suppliers

### Takeaway
A Moroccan entity (or registered individual) exporting digital services must repatriate **100% of export proceeds within 90 days** of performing the service, may keep **up to 70%** of repatriated proceeds in a foreign-currency or convertible-dirham account in a Moroccan bank (85% if "catégorisé"), and may use that account — including an international card backed by it — to pay professional foreign-currency expenses (ads, SaaS, services). Entities without such an account get an e-commerce card capped by tax paid (50,000 MAD for new companies; up to 200,000; 1 M if categorised; 2 M for JEI).

### Cited Findings
- **Definition**: "Les exportations de services désignent … les prestations rendues au Maroc ou à l'étranger par un résident en faveur d'un non-résident et donnant lieu à une rémunération"; must be covered by a service contract. — [IGOC 2026, Chap. IV §4](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Repatriation (art. 97 [derived])**: "Les exportateurs de services sont tenus de procéder au rapatriement du montant intégral des recettes de leurs exportations … dans un délai de quatre-vingt-dix (90) jours à compter de la date de la réalisation des prestations de services." Unpaid receivables: the exporter must pursue recovery and keep the OC informed. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Exporters' accounts (art. 98 [x-ref])**: banks may open "au nom des exportateurs de services, personnes morales ou physiques inscrites au registre du commerce, des comptes en devises ou en dirhams convertibles destinés à leur permettre de régler leurs dépenses professionnelles en devises"; several accounts/banks allowed; holders must use these balances in priority. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Functioning (art. 229 [x-ref])**: credit "70%, au maximum, des recettes d'exportation rapatriées…" (85% for aerospace and OC-categorised operators); unretained amounts can still be credited within one year of the sale of the currency; debit "Toute dépense en devises relative à l'activité professionnelle de l'exportateur et portant sur des opérations, courantes ou en capital, réalisées conformément aux dispositions de la présente Instruction", and some expenses may be paid directly abroad "sous la responsabilité du titulaire du compte, par chèque…, par carte de paiement internationale adossée audit compte", including "Frais afférents à la réalisation d'opérations d'investissement à l'étranger des personnes morales telles que définies par l'article 178 (honoraires, impôts, droits, taxes…)", business travel, "Frais de publicité engagés à l'étranger", "Frais liés aux services en rapport avec son activité". — [IGOC 2026, Chap. VI §3](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **Allowed deductions abroad from export proceeds**: commissions to a foreign representative up to 10% of invoiced amount (contract required), foreign factoring commissions, taxes due in the client's country, expenses needed to execute contracts performed abroad. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- **E-commerce card for entities without FX accounts (art. 121 [derived])**: up to 200,000 MAD/year based on 100% of IS/IR/minimum contribution paid for the last closed year; 50,000 MAD/year for companies paying < 50,000 MAD of tax, exempt companies "et pour les sociétés nouvellement créées"; 1,000,000 MAD for OC-categorised operators; 2,000,000 MAD for JEI; holders of FX accounts: up to their balances. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- Inbound settlement modes: bank transfer from abroad, debit of convertible accounts, international postal orders; "d'autres modes de règlement, tels que … les cartes de paiement internationales peuvent être utilisés dans les conditions définies par la présente Instruction". — [IGOC 2026, Chap. I](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- 2026 liberalisation steps reported: higher dotations for individuals (e-commerce, travel, studies), higher business-travel allowances (1 M MAD for companies without FX accounts; 1.5 M for categorised), wider import-of-services regime with fewer documents, start-up investment derogation; the reform is presented as a shift toward a more declarative regime. — [IGOC 2026 preamble](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf); [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/le-maroc-poursuit-la-liberalisation-progressive-du-regime-de-change/330492); [TelQuel, 5 Jan 2026](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2026/01/05/dotations-et-investissements-voici-ce-qui-change-en-2026_1968744/); [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/463052-change-le-maroc-bascule-vers-un-regime-declaratif.html)

### Inferences
- Architecture A is fully text-supported: Moroccan SARL AU (or registered sole trader) invoices foreign clients, receives wires (or Stripe/PayPal payouts into the Moroccan bank), keeps 70% in USD/EUR in Morocco, pays OpenAI/AWS/ads from the FX account or its card. The binding constraint is practical (which PSPs onboard Moroccan entities), not legal.
- Netting foreign revenue against foreign costs inside a foreign PSP balance without repatriation is "règlement par voie de compensation", listed among operations requiring OC authorisation.
- An unregistered individual (art. 234 account) has a weaker regime than a registered exporter (art. 98/229): no capital operations, no investment costs abroad.

### Gaps
- No specific "statut du freelance exportateur" or dedicated regime for digital-service exporters was found in the IGOC 2026 or in 2025-2026 press; nothing found on a new dirham-flexibility phase changing individual rights in 2025-2026.
- Whether Stripe supports Moroccan entities in 2026 was outside the sources read (adjacent researcher scope).

## 5. Tax: residence, worldwide income, foreign dividends/capital gains, foreign tax credit, declarations (CGI 2026)

### Takeaway
A person with a permanent home, centre of economic interests, or more than 183 days in any 365-day period in Morocco is tax-resident and taxed on worldwide income (CGI art. 23). Foreign-source dividends/interest are taxed at a **flat, final 15% on the gross amount** and foreign-source capital gains on securities at **20% on the gross profit** (art. 73-II-C-2° and F-5°), self-declared and paid **before 1 April** of the following year (arts. 84 bis, 173); other foreign income (e.g., business profits) goes into the progressive scale (top rate 37%), with a treaty-conditioned foreign tax credit (art. 77).

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 23-I**: IR is due by "les personnes physiques qui ont au Maroc leur domicile fiscal, à raison de l'ensemble de leurs revenus et profits, de source marocaine et étrangère". **Art. 23-II**: "une personne physique a son domicile fiscal au Maroc lorsqu'elle a au Maroc son foyer d'habitation permanent, le centre de ses intérêts économiques ou lorsque la durée continue ou discontinue de ses séjours au Maroc dépasse 183 jours pour toute période de 365 jours." — [CGI 2026, art. 23](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 25, 3rd para.**: "Sous réserve des conventions fiscales et des dispositions de l'article 77 ci-dessous, les revenus et profits de source étrangère sont compris dans le revenu global imposable pour leur montant brut, à l'exclusion des revenus et profits de capitaux mobiliers de source étrangère soumis aux taux spécifiques libératoires prévus à l'article 73 (II-C-2° et F-5°)…dans les conditions prévues aux articles 173 et 174". — [CGI 2026, art. 25](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 73-I scale (2026)**: 0% up to 40,000 MAD; 10% 40,001–60,000; 20% 60,001–80,000; 30% 80,001–100,000; 34% 100,001–180,000; 37% above. **Art. 73-II-C-2°**: 15% "pour les revenus bruts de capitaux mobiliers de source étrangère". **Art. 73-II-F-5°**: 20% "pour les profits bruts de capitaux mobiliers de source étrangère". (F-2°: 20% on net gains on unlisted shares generally.) — [CGI 2026, art. 73](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 84 bis**: taxpayers with foreign-source investment income or gains not subject to the art. 174-II-C withholding "doivent souscrire auprès de l'administration fiscale, en même temps que le versement, une déclaration annuelle récapitulant lesdits revenus et profits, avant le 1er avril de l'année qui suit…"; it must be accompanied by supporting documents "et d'une attestation de l'administration fiscale étrangère indiquant la base imposable et le montant de l'impôt acquitté". **Art. 173-I**: tax on foreign-source gross investment income/gains is paid spontaneously before 1 April of the following year. — [CGI 2026, arts. 84 bis, 173](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 77 (foreign tax credit, individuals)**: where foreign income (art. 25 para. 3) was taxed in a source country "avec lequel le Maroc a conclu une convention tendant à éviter la double imposition", the foreign tax "dont le paiement est justifié par le contribuable, est déductible de l'impôt sur le revenu, dans la limite de la fraction de cet impôt correspondant aux revenus étrangers" (tax-sparing recognised if the treaty provides it). — [CGI 2026, art. 77](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Domestic dividends** (for comparison): withholding on Moroccan-source dividends is 11.25% for amounts distributed from 1 Jan 2026 (transitional art. 247-XXXVII-C; 12.5% in 2025); PwC: declining to 10% by 2027. — [CGI 2026, art. 247-XXXVII](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf); [PwC Tax Summaries, reviewed 30 Apr 2026](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/morocco/corporate/withholding-taxes)
- **Foreign dividends received by a Moroccan company**: Moroccan-source dividends "ainsi que ceux de source étrangère sont compris dans les produits financiers de la société bénéficiaire avec un abattement de 100%" (art. 6-I-C-1°). Corporate foreign tax credit: art. 19 bis (treaty countries only, capped at the Moroccan IS on that income). — [CGI 2026, arts. 6, 19 bis](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- Keyword search of the CGI 2026 text for "comptes à l'étranger", "avoirs à l'étranger", "paradis", "direction effective", "établissement stable" returned no hits (see §6). — [CGI 2026](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)

### Inferences
- A resident individual legally holding foreign shares (after OC authorisation) would pay 15% final IR on gross dividends actually received (no deduction; foreign withholding credit only via treaty/art. 77 — note that the 15% is "libératoire" on gross and art. 25 excludes it from global income, so how the credit applies to the flat rate is a technical point to VALIDATE), and 20% on gross gains on sale.
- A dividend route through a Moroccan company is tax-cheaper at the first level: foreign dividend → Moroccan SARL (100% abatement, i.e., no IS) → individual (11.25% in 2026 → 10% in 2027). Combined with the IGOC route (ii)/(iii), this is the coherent "legal holding" chain.
- **Foreign transparent entity (US disregarded LLC)**: the CGI contains no look-through or entity-classification rule for foreign entities (none found). Two readings are possible and neither was found in a DGI circular: (a) the LLC is a company → the member is taxed only on distributions at 15%; (b) the DGI disregards it as the IRS does or applies art. 5/abuse-of-law → the profit is professional income of the individual (scale up to 37%) or income of a Moroccan-taxable enterprise. Because the US treats the LLC's income as the member's and Morocco taxes residents on worldwide income, reading (b) is the prudent planning assumption — VALIDATE.
- No domestic tax form for declaring foreign *accounts* was found in the CGI; the declaration duty for foreign assets sits with the OC (IGOC art. 18 [derived]), while tax declarations concern the *income*.

### Gaps
- DGI circular (note circulaire) treatment of foreign-source professional income of freelancers and of foreign LLCs: not found.
- Whether a salaried employee with additional foreign investment income must also file the annual art. 82 return: not checked in the text.

## 6. When is the foreign company taxable in Morocco? Territoriality, PE/place of management, CFC rules, abuse of law, transfer pricing

### Takeaway
Morocco has **no CFC legislation** in the CGI 2026 text, but it does not need one in this fact pattern: art. 5 taxes any company "whether or not it has a seat in Morocco" on profits from the activity it carries on in Morocco, treaties give Morocco the right to tax a fixed place of business/place of management here, and art. 213-V lets the DGI disregard fictitious or purely tax-driven arrangements. A foreign company whose sole manager works from Morocco is therefore exposed to Moroccan IS (20%) on its profits, plus transfer-pricing scrutiny (art. 213-II, 214-III) of any flows with a related Moroccan entity.

### Cited Findings
- **Art. 5-I**: "Les sociétés, qu'elles aient ou non un siège au Maroc, sont imposables à raison de l'ensemble des produits, bénéfices et revenus : se rapportant aux biens qu'elles possèdent, à l'activité qu'elles exercent et aux opérations lucratives qu'elles réalisent au Maroc, même à titre occasionnel ; dont le droit d'imposition est attribué au Maroc en vertu des conventions tendant à éviter la double imposition". Art. 5-II: non-resident companies are also taxable (withholding) on gross art.-15 income for services rendered to Moroccan persons. — [CGI 2026, art. 5](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 19-I-A**: standard IS rate 20% (35% for companies with net profit ≥ 100 M MAD; 40% banks/insurers); transitional schedule of art. 247-XXXVII converges on 20% for financial years opened from 1 Jan 2026. — [CGI 2026, arts. 19, 247](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf); [Challenge](https://www.challenge.ma/fiscalite-les-nouveaux-taux-de-lis-en-2026-318443/)
- **Art. 213-II (transfer pricing)**: "Lorsqu'une entreprise a directement ou indirectement des liens de dépendance avec des entreprises situées au Maroc ou hors du Maroc, les bénéfices indirectement transférés, soit par voie de majoration ou de diminution des prix d'achat ou de vente, soit par tout autre moyen, sont rapportés au résultat fiscal et/ou au chiffre d'affaires déclarés", determined by comparison with similar enterprises or direct assessment. — [CGI 2026, art. 213](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 213-V (abus de droit)**: "Les opérations constitutives d'un abus de droit ne sont pas opposables à l'administration et peuvent être écartées afin de restituer leur véritable caractère" where they "ont un caractère fictif ou visent uniquement la recherche du bénéfice des avantages fiscaux à l'encontre des objectifs poursuivis par les dispositions législatives" or "visent à éluder l'impôt ou à en réduire le montant qui aurait été normalement supporté eu égard à la situation réelle du contribuable ou de ses activités". — [CGI 2026, art. 213-V](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 214-III-A**: enterprises having transactions with dependent enterprises outside Morocco "doivent communiquer à l'administration fiscale, par procédé électronique, la documentation permettant de justifier leur politique de prix de transfert" (master file + local file; list and modalities set by regulation). A December 2025 decree on electronic filing of transfer-pricing files is mentioned in OECD/press material. — [CGI 2026, art. 214](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- The terms "direction effective" and "établissement stable" do not appear in the CGI 2026 text (keyword search); these concepts come from the treaties (Art. 4(3) tie-breaker for companies; Art. 5 PE incl. "siège de direction"). — [CGI 2026](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)

### Inferences
- Domestic law reaches the foreign company via "l'activité qu'elles exercent au Maroc" (art. 5): if development, sales, management and decision-making are performed in Morocco by the resident owner, the DGI can assert that the profit is attributable to a Moroccan activity/establishment and levy IS at 20% (plus dividend-level tax and penalties), independently of any CFC rule. Under OECD/UN-model treaties a "place of management" in Morocco is a PE, and many Moroccan treaties resolve dual corporate residence by place of effective management.
- A foreign subsidiary held legally by a Moroccan company (routes ii/iii) needs real foreign substance (local director/staff/functions) or, failing that, an arm's-length intercompany arrangement where the Moroccan company invoices the subsidiary for the work done in Morocco (cost-plus or profit-split) so that the profit attributable to Moroccan functions is taxed in Morocco. That is the defensible structure: the foreign entity acts as contracting/payment-collection entity with a routine margin, the Moroccan company earns the entrepreneurial return as export revenue (repatriated; 70% FX retention).
- Art. 213-V makes "structure exists only to avoid Moroccan tax" a direct attack vector; business reasons (PSP access, US customers' contracting requirements, investor requirements) should be documented.

### Gaps
- No Moroccan case law or DGI doctrine was found on PE/place-of-management reclassification of a foreign company run by a Moroccan resident; this is inference from statute and treaty models — VALIDATE.
- Morocco's MLI positions (PPT, Art. 4 tie-breaker choices) were not retrieved.

## 7. Transparency: CRS, FATCA, OC–DGI data sharing

### Takeaway
As of the OECD's 30 September 2026 status table, **Morocco has still not started CRS exchanges**: it voluntarily committed to 2025, missed it, and is now "expected to commence exchanges by 2028 at the latest". FATCA has applied in Morocco since 2018 but that reports US persons' accounts in Morocco to the US, not Moroccans' US accounts to Morocco. Detection today relies on OC bank reporting, targeted controls, a dedicated digital-transactions unit, and OC↔DGI and foreign-partner exchanges on request.

### Cited Findings
- "Morocco voluntarily committed to commence exchanges in 2025 but has not yet exchanged. In 2025, Morocco was also identified as a jurisdiction relevant for the implementation of the AEOI Standard through the Global Forum process. This process identifies 2028 as the appropriate year for first exchanges under the AEOI Standard and Morocco is therefore expected to commence exchanges by 2028 at the latest." (Last update: 30 September 2026.) — [OECD, Status of AEOI commitments](https://www.oecd.org/tax/transparency/documents/AEOI-commitments.pdf)
- Morocco signed the CRS multilateral competent authority agreement on 25 June 2019; delay attributed to unfinished legislative framework and technical infrastructure. — [CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/mar/news-information/l-echange-automatique-d-informations-au-maroc); [Bladi, 5 Dec 2025](https://www.bladi.net/echange-fiscal-automatique-reporte-maroc,118248.html); [BDO](https://bdo.global/en-gb/insights/tax/transfer-pricing/morocco-postpones-entry-into-force-of-automatic-exchange-of-financial-and-tax-information-agreemen)
- Morocco already provides financial information to partners on request. — [Médias24, 5 Jul 2023](https://medias24.com/2023/07/05/secret-bancaire-le-maroc-fournit-deja-des-renseignements-financiers-a-ses-partenaires-internationaux/)
- FATCA: decree-law published in the Bulletin Officiel in March 2018; reported as officially in force in Morocco in September 2018. — [Médias24, 1 Mar 2018](https://medias24.com/2018/03/01/le-decret-loi-sur-la-fatca-americaine-publie-au-b-o/); [Médias24, 10 Sep 2018](https://medias24.com/2018/09/10/evasion-fiscale-la-loi-americaine-fatca-entre-officiellement-en-vigueur-au-maroc/)
- OC created a dedicated service for controlling online foreign-currency earners in 2018 and a special cell for monitoring digital transactions (reported Feb 2025). — [FNH, 17 Jan 2022](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/office-des-changes-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours); [SNRTnews (snippet only)](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/comment-loffice-des-changes-controle-les-revenus-des-createurs-de-contenu-et-des)
- OC sent the DGI a list of 53 cases of excessive spending abroad (Nov 2025) and uses electronic information exchange with foreign partner administrations; OC is digitalising foreign-asset controls/declarations. — [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/449830-449830.html); [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/493721-avoirs-a-letranger-loffice-des-changes-mise-sur-le-numerique-pour-renforcer-les-controles.html)
- Banks must file periodic declarations to the OC on all operations executed under the IGOC. — [IGOC 2026, Chap. I §5](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)

### Inferences
- The absence of CRS inflows until (at latest) 2028 does not make an unauthorised structure lawful; it only describes current detection probability. With no limitation period reported for exchange offences, data arriving in 2028+ can expose earlier years. The principal's "verified, legally defensible" requirement rules out relying on this gap.
- Under the FATCA Model 1 framework reciprocity toward Morocco is not established by the sources found; do not assume the IRS reports US LLC bank accounts to Morocco, and do not assume it never will (US Corporate Transparency/BOI data is another channel, outside this scope).

### Gaps
- Morocco's exact FATCA IGA status on the US Treasury list (Model 1 "in force" vs "agreement in substance") was not verified on treasury.gov.
- Status of the Moroccan CRS implementing law (bill 77-19 was cited in older press) as of Nov 2026: not confirmed.

## 8. Double tax treaties: dividend rates and coverage

### Takeaway
Morocco has treaties with the US, UK, UAE, Ireland, France, Singapore and most EU states; PwC's 2026 table shows no treaty entry for **Estonia**, **Hong Kong** or **Cyprus**. Treaty dividend caps are typically 10–15% for individuals (UAE 10/5, Ireland 10/6, Singapore 10/8, US and UK 15/10, France 15).

### Cited Findings
- Treaty dividend withholding rates (individuals & non-qualifying companies / qualifying companies): United States 15/10; United Kingdom 15/10; UAE 10/5; Ireland 10/6; France 15/15; Singapore 10/8; Netherlands 15/10; Luxembourg 15/10; Malta 10/6.5; Switzerland 15/7; Canada 15/10; Germany 15/5; Spain 15/10; Portugal 15/10. Estonia, Hong Kong and Cyprus are not in the list. Domestic non-treaty dividend WHT 11.25% for FY 2026, declining to 10% by 2027. Page last reviewed 30 April 2026. — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries – Morocco, Withholding taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/morocco/corporate/withholding-taxes)
- UAE–Morocco convention (1999): dividends 5–10%; UAE residents need a tax residency certificate from the UAE FTA. — [Upsilon Consulting](https://upsilon-consulting.com/en/morocco-uae-tax-convention/)
- US–Morocco treaty dates from 1977 (pre-modern model). — [IRS treaty text (archive)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090115092228/http://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/morocco.pdf)
- Foreign tax credit in Morocco is only available for tax paid in a **treaty** country (arts. 77 and 19 bis CGI). — [CGI 2026](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)

### Inferences
- The PwC table shows rates applied by Morocco on outbound dividends; treaty caps are generally reciprocal, so they indicate the maximum source-country tax on dividends paid to a Moroccan resident (in practice UK and UAE levy no dividend WHT domestically; Estonia taxes distributions at company level; the US applies 30% absent treaty → 15%/10% under the treaty). 
- For a non-treaty jurisdiction (Estonia/Hong Kong per PwC list), no Moroccan credit is available for foreign tax under arts. 77/19 bis → potential unrelieved double taxation; treaty jurisdictions are preferable for a legally held subsidiary.

### Gaps
- Residence tie-breaker wording (individuals Art. 4(2); companies Art. 4(3) — POEM vs mutual agreement) for each treaty was not read; the DGI's official convention list (tax.gov.ma) was inaccessible (403). Whether a Morocco–Estonia treaty is signed/in force must be checked on the DGI list — VALIDATE.
- LLC treaty eligibility (a disregarded LLC is not itself a US "resident" for treaty purposes) not researched here.

## 9. Moroccan-entity options for Architecture A (tax side)

### Takeaway
Since the 2023-2026 IS reform the ordinary company rate is a flat **20%** (2026) with **no remaining general export-specific reduced rate or 5-year export holiday** in the text read; 5-year total IS exemptions survive for CFC-status service companies, for "externalisation de services" (offshoring) companies and designated industrial activities, after which 20% applies. The auto-entrepreneur regime (1% on services up to 200,000 MAD) is too small and structurally awkward for a scalable export business.

### Cited Findings
- IS 20% standard rate (art. 19-I-A), reached for financial years opened from 1 Jan 2026 under art. 247-XXXVII-A. — [CGI 2026](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 6-II-B-4°**: total IS exemption "pendant les cinq (5) premiers exercices consécutifs à compter de la date du début de leur exploitation" for "les sociétés industrielles exerçant des activités fixées par voie réglementaire ; et les sociétés exerçant les activités d'externalisation de services à l'intérieur ou en dehors des plateformes industrielles intégrées dédiées à ces activités, conformément aux textes législatifs et réglementaires en vigueur". — [CGI 2026, art. 6](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 6-II-B-6°**: service companies with CFC status get total IS exemption for five consecutive financial years from the first year of the status; "cette exonération cesse d'être appliquée à l'expiration des soixante (60) premiers mois qui suivent la date de constitution des sociétés concernées". CFC companies are excluded from the 35% rate (art. 19-I-B). — [CGI 2026](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- **Art. 73-II-F-9°**: 20% IR on gross salaries of employees of CFC-status companies "pour une période maximale de dix (10) ans" from taking up their functions (option for the normal scale possible). — [CGI 2026, art. 73](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- Export-company 5-year exemption abolished (LF 2020) according to an accounting firm's 2026 guide; ZAI and CFC: 5-year exemption then 20% permanently; the former 15% specific rate repealed. — [Upsilon Consulting, Exonérations d'IS 2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/exonerations-is-maroc/)
- **Auto-entrepreneur** (arts. 42 bis, 42 ter, 73-III): turnover caps 500,000 MAD (commerce/industry/crafts) and 200,000 MAD (services); tax on cashed turnover (0.5% for commercial activities per art. 73-III; services tier 1% — second line not displayed in the extract read); "lorsque le chiffre d'affaires annuel au titre des prestations de service réalisées, pour le compte d'un même client, dépasse quatre-vingt mille (80 000) dirhams, le surplus est soumis à l'impôt sur le revenu, par voie de retenue à la source opérée par ledit client au taux prévu à l'article 73 (II-G-8°)" (G = 30%); option lost if caps exceeded two consecutive years; must join social security. — [CGI 2026](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- Dividends from the Moroccan company to the individual: 11.25% withholding in 2026 (→10% in 2027). — [CGI 2026 art. 247-XXXVII-C](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf); [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/morocco/corporate/withholding-taxes)
- Exchange side: companies in ZAIs and offshore financial places are **non-residents** for exchange control (free FX accounts), and CFC-status companies hold FX accounts and may pay online services up to their balances. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Baseline for Architecture A: SARL AU, IS 20% on net profit, then 11.25%/10% on dividends → combined ≈ 29% (2026) / 28% (2027) on distributed profit; undistributed profit stays at 20%. A reasonable manager's salary is deductible but taxed on the IR scale (up to 37%) plus social charges.
- An AI/SaaS exporter might qualify as "externalisation de services" (offshoring) for the 5-year IS holiday only if its activity falls within the regulatory list of offshoring activities (ITO/BPO/KPO etc.) — eligibility of a product/SaaS company (as opposed to outsourced services) is uncertain — VALIDATE.
- CFC status is designed for regional HQs, financial firms and professional-services providers with international focus; eligibility and cost for a one-person digital company are doubtful.

### Gaps
- CFC eligibility categories, fees and substance requirements (CFC Authority site) not retrieved. 
- The offshoring-employee IR cap (historically an effective 20% in Casanearshore/Technopolis) is **not** in the CGI 2026 text read (art. 73-II-F-7° and 8° are "abrogé"); whether it survives through another instrument is unverified.
- Tamwilcom / Innov Invest funding terms and ADD-label non-FX benefits: not researched (no source read).
- VAT treatment of exported services (exemption with right to deduct, art. 92 CGI) not checked in the text.

## 10. Legal ways for the individual to actually hold a foreign structure; change of residence; returning residents

### Takeaway
Four lawful paths exist: (i) a specific OC authorisation (discretionary, no published grant practice for individuals); (ii) through a Moroccan company meeting the art. 179 conditions (3 years + certified accounts, ≤200 M MAD/yr, activity-related); (iii) through an ADD-labelled JEI (≤10 M MAD/yr, no seniority/audit condition; founder "flip" if firm foreign funding); (iv) genuinely ceasing to be a Moroccan tax resident — exchange-control status follows tax residence by definition. Law 63-14 governs assets held abroad by Moroccans who transfer their tax residence (back) to Morocco.

### Cited Findings
- Routes (i)–(iii): see §1 and §2 quotations. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- Route (iv): exchange-control "non-résident" = "Personne physique considérée comme non-résidente au sens de la législation fiscale en vigueur"; tax residence = permanent home, centre of economic interests, or >183 days in any 365-day period in Morocco (any one criterion suffices). — [IGOC 2026, Définitions](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf); [CGI 2026, art. 23-II](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)
- Returning Moroccans: the IGOC repeatedly refers to "les marocains ayant résidé à l'étranger et déclaré leurs avoirs et liquidités détenus à l'étranger conformément aux dispositions de la loi 63-14 et des textes pris pour son application", who keep FX/convertible accounts in Morocco, may use them for e-commerce up to their balance, place the balances, and may even repay consumer credits contracted abroad while they resided there. — [IGOC 2026, Chap. IV §7, Chap. V §6](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- New residents entering into possession of foreign assets: declaration to the OC within 3 months (art. 18 [derived]), "sauf dispositions règlementaires particulières". — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- Tax on departure: art. 27 CGI addresses the determination of global income of persons who establish or cease their tax domicile in Morocco (foreign-source income counted from the day of establishment / up to the date of cessation). — [CGI 2026, art. 27](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)

### Inferences
- Route (iv) requires that *none* of the three art. 23 criteria remain: a salaried job in Morocco and a family home there are, respectively, a centre of economic interests and a permanent home — a person who keeps them remains resident whatever days are spent abroad. It is a real relocation (new country of tax residence, MRE status with consular registration, Moroccan accounts converted to non-resident/convertible status), not a paper exercise. A company formed abroad while non-resident and assets built abroad can lawfully be kept on return if declared under law 63-14 — VALIDATE details (deadline, scope, whether an operating company's shares are covered, subsequent income repatriation).
- For a salaried employee who intends to stay in Morocco, the realistic sequencing is: Architecture A now (Moroccan SARL AU exporting) → apply for the ADD JEI label → foreign subsidiary under the JEI derogation if a foreign contracting/PSP entity is commercially needed → otherwise wait for the 3-year/audit route.

### Gaps
- Text of law 63-14 and its decree (deadlines, covered assets, tax effects) not read.
- OC practice on authorisations for individuals: no data. MRE-status formalities at the OC: not found in the IGOC beyond definitions.

## 11. Enforcement reality for freelancers / online entrepreneurs (reputable sources only)

### Takeaway
Reputable sources document that the OC has, since 2018, specifically targeted individuals earning foreign currency online and has sanctioned non-repatriation, unauthorised foreign companies/assets, and foreign accounts including PayPal/Payoneer; aggregate control statistics show individuals are a growing share of cases. No named, published case of a freelancer sanctioned specifically for a US LLC + Stripe was found.

### Cited Findings
- OC DG (MAP interview): dedicated control service created in 2018; risk-based controls of resident individuals with online foreign-currency income; sanctions imposed for non-repatriation, assets abroad without authorisation ("création de sociétés", real estate, financial assets, bitcoin), foreign accounts incl. PayPal/Payoneer, and compensation; reminder of the 90-day repatriation rule; reference to Circular 3/2020 creating FX facilities for unregistered individuals with foreign-source income. — [FNH, 17 Jan 2022](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/office-des-changes-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours)
- OC special cell for controlling and monitoring digital transactions; article on how the OC controls content creators' and freelancers' income (Feb 2025). — [SNRTnews (snippet only)](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/comment-loffice-des-changes-controle-les-revenus-des-createurs-de-contenu-et-des)
- 2025: 46 of 104 judicial requisitions concerned individuals (26 in 2024); unauthorised foreign assets of individuals a main focus. — [TelQuel, 27 Jul 2026 (snippet only)](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2026/07/27/les-controles-de-loffice-des-changes-engrange-723-milliards-de-dh-en-2025_2000870/)
- OC scrutiny of Moroccan outbound investments (Gulf, Africa) and of investors abroad. — [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/423547-investissements-a-letranger-loffice-des-changes-renforce-son-controle-sur-les-flux-vers-le-golfe-et-lafrique.html); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/societe/fuite-de-capitaux-loffice-des-changes-serre-la-vis-autour-des-investisseurs-a-letranger_UJ6SJYT3XBALZBY6ENDBHJUN6Q/)

### Inferences
- Enforcement is real but statistically concentrated on larger amounts (20% of fines > 1 M MAD); small operators are mostly caught via bank-flow anomalies (card spending, inbound transfers without export documentation) and, in future, CRS.

## 12. Employment constraint: can a private-sector employee be shareholder / gérant of a Moroccan or foreign company?

### Takeaway
No source read identifies a statutory prohibition in Moroccan law on a private-sector employee holding shares in, or managing, a company; constraints come from the employment contract (exclusivity, non-compete, loyalty) and, for civil servants, from the public-service statute. This section is weakly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Exclusivity clauses can prohibit an employee from working for other employers or carrying on competing activities; "le législateur n'a prévu aucun texte de loi régissant ce genre de clause" and Moroccan case law is not uniform. — [La Vie éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/?p=50704)
- Debate on the dual role of corporate officer and employee in Moroccan SAs. — [Médias24, 20 Mar 2024 (title only)](https://medias24.com/2024/03/20/gouvernance-des-s-a-dirigeant-et-salarie-une-double-casquette-fortement-remise-en-question/)

### Inferences
- Being a passive shareholder is generally compatible with employment; being gérant of one's own SARL AU is a contractual question (check exclusivity, non-compete, IP-assignment and conflict-of-interest clauses, employer's internal code; obtain written consent where needed). Under exchange control, the employee status is irrelevant except for the art. 185 employee-share-plan case.

### Gaps
- Labour Code (loi 65-99) provisions on loyalty/competition and the civil-service incompatibility rule (dahir of 24 Feb 1958, art. 15) were not read; CNSS consequences of being a gérant while salaried elsewhere not researched — VALIDATE with a Moroccan labour lawyer.

## Summary matrix (for the strategist)

### Takeaway
Permitted without authorisation: Moroccan company/registered individual exporting services with 90-day full repatriation and 70% FX retention; Moroccan company with 3 years + certified accounts investing abroad (≤200 M MAD/yr); ADD-labelled JEI investing abroad (≤10 M MAD/yr). Requires prior OC authorisation: any foreign company, account or asset held by a resident individual; holdings; compensation. Must be declared: foreign assets entering a resident's patrimony (3 months, OC); foreign-source investment income (before 1 April, DGI). Profits reach the individual lawfully as Moroccan salary/dividends (A) or as foreign dividends repatriated within 30 days via the Moroccan parent (B-legal).

### Cited Findings
- All rules above are sourced in sections 1–10. — [IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf); [CGI 2026](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/documents/cgi-2026-maroc.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Architecture B held directly by the individual (US LLC etc. formed online) = not permitted without OC authorisation**, regardless of amount; additionally exposed to Moroccan IS/IR via art. 5/23/213-V. Not a defensible architecture.
- **Architecture B held via a Moroccan company** (JEI now, or ordinary company after 3 audited years) = permitted; profits may be retained in the subsidiary; dividends must be repatriated within 30 days and are received by the Moroccan parent with 100% abatement; transfer pricing and PE/substance must be managed.
- **Architecture A** = fully permitted today; cost ≈ 20% IS + 11.25%/10% dividend tax.

### Gaps
- Priority validations with a Moroccan tax lawyer / the OC: (1) written OC position on an individual holding a zero-capital foreign LLC; (2) JEI label eligibility and bank practice for the 10 M MAD derogation; (3) tax characterisation of a disregarded LLC; (4) PE/place-of-management exposure and intercompany pricing; (5) law 63-14 mechanics for a future return after genuine expatriation; (6) exact sanctions text of the 1949 dahir; (7) IGOC article numbers marked [derived].
