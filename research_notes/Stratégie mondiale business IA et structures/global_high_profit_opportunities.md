# Global high-net-profit AI-operated / AI-leveraged online business models for solo owners (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-12. Evidence labels used throughout: **[VP]** verified-primary (official vendor/regulator page or payment-verified feed), **[SR]** self-reported by founder/company, **[SEC]** secondary (press, blog, aggregator, third-party estimate).
Method caveat: most data points below were obtained from search-result summaries of the linked pages; only the RevenueCat/PPC Land article was opened in full. Two fetches failed (Indie Hackers 403, BigIdeasDB 429). Figures labelled [SEC] should be re-checked on the linked page before being quoted as fact in a final report.

## Objective: catalogue of 20 concrete opportunities (ranked by strength of small-operator evidence, not by hype)

Only a handful of models have documented small-team outcomes at or above $5-20k/month net: B2B app-ecosystem apps (Shopify, Atlassian), productized high-ticket subscriptions sold inbound, AI-visibility (GEO) tracking, and acquisition of an already-profitable small SaaS at ~2-3.5x profit. Most "AI agent for local business" and compliance-service ideas show public price points but NO verified solo-operator revenue; they are plausible but unproven.

- Tier A (documented small-team outcomes at or above target, with built-in distribution and no sales calls): #1 Shopify apps, #2 Atlassian Forge apps, #5 productized subscription, #6 acquisition. Tier B (demand and prices visible, small-operator profit unproven): #3, #4, #7, #8, #9, #11, #12. Tier C (poor base rates or structural blockers): #13, #14, #18, #19, #20. Tier P (monetising pharma expertise personally, high hourly value, low scalability, employer-conflict risk): #15, #16, #17.
- Gross margins: software models (#1-4, #10-14) are typically 80%+ before paid acquisition; this is a general industry assumption, not a figure verified in this round. The only margin claim found was "~80% margin" for AI voice agencies, self-reported and undated ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-profitable-ai-voice-saas-agency-300-800-mrr-per-client-frAbgO1yQMfHOFFtY3gE)).

Whether each platform onboards a Moroccan individual/entity was NOT verified here.
- Defensibility against "customer just uses ChatGPT/Claude": strongest where value is embedded in a host platform's data and permissions (#1, #2), where a legal role or signed accountability is sold (#8, #9), or where continuous multi-engine measurement is needed (#3); weakest for content-like deliverables (#16, #18) and thin wrappers (#13, #14).

## Vertical AI agents / vertical SaaS for US SMB trades and professional services

### Takeaway
Price points of $300-2,000/month for AI receptionists are widely advertised, but this research found zero verified solo-operator revenue; the visible "winners" are sellers of white-label platforms and courses. Outbound AI calling is legally constrained by the FCC's February 2024 ruling.

### Cited Findings
- An Indie Hackers community post describes an AI-voice agency model of $1-2K setup + $300-800 MRR per client at ~80% margin on a white-label platform (self-reported, undated) [SR] — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-profitable-ai-voice-saas-agency-300-800-mrr-per-client-frAbgO1yQMfHOFFtY3gE)
- A white-label AI receptionist built in GoHighLevel is offered to agencies at $1,999/mo [SR, sales listing] — [Skool](https://www.skool.com/agencyworld/seeking-agency-partners-white-label-ai-voice-receptionist-built-in-ghl)
- Common stack: Retell or Vapi for voice, Twilio, GoHighLevel/n8n, integrations with Housecall Pro/Jobber/ServiceTitan; advice to white-label until 10+ clients [SEC, vendor blog] — [GrowwStacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/how-to-build-ai-receptionist-hvac-business/)
- Agencies are being taught to sell AI receptionists to local businesses by cold call, i.e. the channel in practice is outbound sales [SEC, vendor blog] — [GrowwStacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/sell-ai-receptionists-to-local-businesses-with-cold-calls/)
- FCC Declaratory Ruling of 8 Feb 2024: AI-generated/cloned voices are "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the TCPA; callers need prior express consent (prior express written consent for telemarketing), must identify themselves and offer opt-out; effective immediately; not a blanket ban [SEC, law firms summarising primary] — [Hunton](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/fcc-issues-declaratory-ruling-that-tcpa-applies-to-ai-generated-voice-calls); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/insights/2024/02/Did-the-FCC-Really-Ban-AI-Generated-Voice-Calls-No-But-Restrictions-Apply); [Wiley](https://wiley.law/alert-FCC-Extends-Regulatory-Reach-Over-AI-Announces-TCPA-Restrictions-Cover-AI-Generated-Voices-in-Outbound-Calls)
- The ruling empowers the FCC and state attorneys general to pursue unsolicited AI-voice robocalls [SEC] — [Akin](https://akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/proposal-on-ai-generated-robocalls-reflects-fccs-focus-on-artificial-intelligence)

### Inferences
- An AI voice agent cannot lawfully cold-call US prospects to sell the product without prior express written consent; "let an AI agent do the sales calls" is therefore not a compliant acquisition channel in the US. A commission-only human partner or inbound channels (integration marketplaces of Jobber/ServiceTitan-type software, local SEO) are the realistic options. This is an inference from the ruling's scope.
- The ruling addresses calls placed with an artificial voice; an inbound answering product (the business's own customers calling in) appears to sit outside that restriction, but this reading was not confirmed against a legal source in this round.
- Because the product is assembled from commodity components (Retell/Vapi + GHL), defensibility is low and competition is intense; margin accrues to whoever owns distribution.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue for solo vendors in dental, legal intake, property management, insurance agency, accounting, medical billing/prior-auth or restaurant phone ordering was found. HIPAA (BAA requirements) and state-level AI-call laws were not researched.
- a16z/Bessemer/YC vertical-AI lists were not consulted; those lists cover funded companies, not solo operators.

## App-ecosystem businesses with built-in distribution

### Takeaway
This is the best-evidenced route to five-figure monthly profit without sales calls: marketplaces supply search-driven demand, handle billing, and currently take 0% on the first $1M (Shopify, Atlassian Forge). Distribution is highly skewed, and no authoritative median exists.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify: developers keep 100% of the first $1,000,000 in lifetime gross app revenue (counting revenue from 1 Jan 2025) and 85% above; companies with $20M+ app earnings or $100M+ revenue pay 15% on all [VP] — [Shopify dev docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)
- Earlier 2025 wording described the $1M exemption as annual; the docs now say lifetime, so the benefit was reduced [VP, change date not established] — [Shopify dev docs (mirror)](https://shopify-dev.shopifycloud.com/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)
- Zigpoll (Jason Zigelbaum): $125k MRR as a solo founder; Shopify App Store about one third of new signups [SR] — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/hitting-125k-mrr-as-a-solo-founder-by-doubling-down-on-the-right-segment-c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO)
- WideBundle (Mat De Sousa): about $25k/month within a year [SR, older story] — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-we-validated-grew-a-shopify-app-to-25k-mo-in-less-than-a-year-159f82699c)
- Judge.me (PJ Celis): bootstrapped to $13M ARR (team-based) [SR] — [Foreplay podcast](https://www.foreplay.co/post/e26-the-bootstrapped-playbook-for-dominating-the-shopify-app-store-with-pj-celis)
- Portfolio of Shopify apps bootstrapped to $28k MRR [SR] — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/RJqmHvigMN2t5VsTM1hR)
- Third-party modelled estimates: top 25% of Shopify app developers ~ $167k/yr, average ~ $93k, ~0.18% above $1M/yr; another estimate gives median monthly app revenue from ~$191 (youngest) to ~$19,098 (oldest apps). These are estimates from public signals, not billing data [SEC, low reliability] — [Barn2](https://barn2.com/?p=712947); [Koala Apps](https://koala-apps.io/blog/shopify-store-benchmarks/)
- Atlassian: 0% revenue share on Forge app revenue until a partner reaches $1M lifetime Forge revenue, from 1 Jan 2026; standard Forge rate 16% from 1 Jan 2026 and 17% from 1 Jul 2026; incentive is described as temporary [VP] — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developers/runs-on-atlassian-apps-can-now-take-home-100-of-marketplace-revenue); [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developers/updates-to-marketplace-revenue-share-2026)
- Atlassian Marketplace described as a $7 billion marketplace; as of end June 2026 more than 40% of Atlassian customers had at least one Marketplace app installed [VP] — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/teamwork/atlassian-marketplace-7-billion)
- Solo developer Vitalii Zurian (Lizard Brain) went from side project (2014) to full-time with 10 Marketplace apps; no revenue disclosed [VP profile] — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-turned-atlassian-marketplace-app-entrepreneur)
- Atlassian has a forum thread on publishing paid apps as an individual without a registered company (verification requirements) [VP forum] — [Atlassian developer community](https://community.developer.atlassian.com/t/individual-developer-no-registered-company-whats-the-minimum-verification-needed-to-publish-a-paid-via-atlassian-app/102404)
- WordPress: Freemius says a maker on its platform earned an average of $43,000 annually (2024 review) [SR] — [Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/year-in-review-2024/)
- WordPress: Barn2's 2025 transparency report notes a meaningful drop in new plugin sales; a survey of 33 plugin companies found 80% flat or down [SR/SEC, small sample] — [Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/); [JK Dev Studio](https://jkdevstudio.com/plugin-sales-did-not-drop/)
- Chrome extensions: verified examples are small (SEC-filing PDF tool ~$24 MRR Stripe-verified; AI Toolbox ~$929 MRR verified) [VP via TrustMRR] — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/sec-filing-pdf-generator); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ai-toolbox)

### Inferences
- With AI coding agents lowering build cost, the scarce asset in app stores is ranking and reviews, not code; older apps earn far more (per the modelled age gradient), which favours buying an established listing over launching a new one.
- Atlassian has fewer, higher-value buyers and enterprise per-seat pricing; Shopify has more buyers and more competitors. Both allow a hands-off, inbound, no-call model.
- The WordPress plugin channel is weakening in 2025; it looks less attractive than Shopify/Atlassian for a new entrant.

### Gaps
- No official earnings distribution from Shopify or Atlassian; no data found for Salesforce AppExchange, HubSpot, Microsoft AppSource, Zendesk, QuickBooks/Xero, Figma/Notion/Webflow/Framer in this round.
- Whether Shopify Partners and Atlassian pay out to a Morocco-resident individual or entity was not verified.

## Compliance-deadline-driven products

### Takeaway
Prices are public and demand is deadline-driven, but no small-vendor profit evidence was found for 2025-2026; one category (US Corporate Transparency filings) was effectively eliminated by regulation, and one (accessibility overlays) carries proven enforcement risk.

### Cited Findings
- GPSR EU Responsible Person: typical EUR 150-500/year for basic service; per-brand unlimited-SKU plans EUR 600-2,500/year; a UK-to-EU provider lists GBP 1,440-4,320/year; own EU entity alternative EUR 3,000-15,000/year [SEC, vendor pages] — [EUverify](https://euverify.com/resource/authorised-representative-costs-guide/); [eugpsr](https://eugpsr.pages.dev/blog/gpsr-cost-pricing-guide)
- One provider claims "300+ brands" [SR] — [24hour-AR](https://hs.24hour-ar.com/lp/uk-to-eu)
- EAA audit pricing: EUR 2,500-5,000 for a 15-20 page site, EUR 3,000-6,500 for 20 pages WCAG 2.2 AA, remediation EUR 5,000-20,000; automated scans flag only ~25% of issues [SEC, vendor blogs] — [Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/eaa-accessibility-audit-remediation-cost-dublin-2026); [Accessible.org](https://accessible.org/eaa-compliance-cost/)
- EAA generally does not apply directly to B2B-only sellers [SEC] — [Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/european-accessibility-act-b2b-website-compliance-cost-2026)
- FTC ordered accessiBe to pay $1 million for misrepresenting that its AI overlay made any website WCAG-compliant; final order approved April 2025, 20-year compliance reporting [VP/SEC] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/03/ftc-orders-ai-accessibility-startup-accessibe-to-pay-1m-for-misleading-advertising); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/88115)
- A critic cites accessiBe 2024 revenue of $51.3M (shows market size, not small-operator proof) [SEC] — [Adrian Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2025/01/ftc-catches-up-to-accessibe.html)
- Cookie consent: CookieFirst closed EUR 885K revenue in 2021 with ~5,000 paying customers, team of nine, bootstrapped [SR, dated] — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/cookiefirst/interviews/cookiefirst-tom-van-den-bos-2022)
- Compliance automation: Octave-X (bootstrapped) sells Certus at $299/month for HIPAA/GDPR/SOC 2 monitoring; no revenue disclosed [SR] — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/octave-x/)
- US Corporate Transparency Act: FinCEN interim final rule effective 26 March 2025 exempts domestic companies and US persons from beneficial-ownership reporting; only ~12,000 foreign reporting companies expected to file [SEC, law firms] — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/fincen-eliminates-beneficial-ownership-reporting-requirements-us-companies); [Greenberg Traurig](https://gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/3/cta-update-fincen-issues-interim-final-rule-exempting-domestic-companies-and-us-beneficial-owners-from-reporting-requirements)

### Inferences
- CTA/BOI filing services are a cautionary example: a deadline-driven business can vanish with one rule change. Deadline products should be judged on mandate durability.
- GPSR responsible-person service needs roughly 100-300 clients at EUR 150-500 to reach EUR 5k+/month, requires an EU-established legal person, and carries product-liability-adjacent exposure; it is a legal-role business rather than an AI product, which makes it resistant to "just use ChatGPT" but also not hands-off.
- Accessibility work that sells a human-signed audit is defensible; fully automated "compliance" claims are exactly what the FTC sanctioned.

### Gaps
- No small-vendor evidence found for e-invoicing mandates, EU AI Act, NIS2, CSRD, EUDR, CBAM, US sales tax, or Amazon seller compliance (another researcher covers country regulation). No independent price survey for audits.

## Data / API businesses at scale

### Takeaway
GEO/AI-visibility tracking is the fastest-growing data category with real revenue, but leaders are VC-funded and well capitalised; the one bootstrapped example is sub-$1M. Apify payouts are growing quickly in aggregate, but the per-developer distribution is undisclosed.

### Cited Findings
- Peec AI: launched Feb 2025, reported $4M ARR within ten months and a $21M Series A [SR via press release] — [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/berlins-peec-ai-rockets-to-4m-arr-in-ten-months-raises-21m-series-a-to-open-new-york-office)
- Peec estimated at $11.5M ARR in June 2026, up from $5M in December; total funding ~$29M [SEC estimate] — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/peec/)
- Seven GEO startups raised $472M, of which Profound $334.5M [SEC] — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/charts/generative-engine-optimization-startups)
- Incumbents (Ahrefs, Semrush, Conductor) are pushing back against GEO pure-plays [SEC] — [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/ahrefs-semrush-and-conductor-push-back-as-geo-pure-plays-raise-hundreds-of-millions/)
- Otterly.AI: bootstrapped, Vienna, ~$770K 2025 revenue, team 7, pricing from $29/month (Latka estimate, not company-confirmed) [SEC] — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/otterly.ai/customers); [Tooldirectory](https://tooldirectory.ai/tools/otterly-ai)
- Apify distributed over $563,000 to community developers in September 2025 (6x year on year); over $760,000 in January 2026; more than $4 million cumulative [SR by platform] — [EIN Presswire](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/863598206/apify-bets-1m-on-independent-developers-building-ai-s-missing-tools); [NatLawReview press release](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/apify-bets-1m-independent-developers-building-ais-missing-tools)
- Apify monthly payout mechanics are documented officially [VP] — [Apify docs](https://docs.apify.com/actors/monetize/monthly-payouts.md)
- hiQ v. LinkedIn: Ninth Circuit held scraping public data likely does not violate the CFAA, but the district court found hiQ breached LinkedIn's user agreement (including fake accounts); the December 2022 settlement included a permanent injunction, data deletion and $500,000 [SEC, law firms] — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/)

### Inferences
- A solo entrant cannot out-spend Profound/Peec on a horizontal GEO tracker; the open space is a narrow vertical or language/region variant, or an agency-facing white-label. Otterly's ~$770K with seven people implies modest per-head profit even for a recognised bootstrapped player.
- Apify payouts of ~$0.5-0.8M/month spread across an unknown number of developers do not prove any individual reaches $5k+/month; the earlier-round estimate of $0.1-1.7k/month remains unrefuted.
- Scraping products that require logged-in access or breach terms of service carry contract and injunction risk even where CFAA liability is unlikely.

### Gaps
- Meta v. Bright Data (2024), GDPR treatment of scraped personal data, and EU sui generis database right were not sourced in this round. No evidence found on small vendors licensing datasets to AI companies or hedge funds, nor on lead-database/enrichment solo operators. Apify's exact revenue-share percentage was not confirmed on a primary page.

## Productized high-ticket services with a thin human layer

### Takeaway
The productized subscription sold entirely inbound and asynchronous (no calls) is proven at $1M+/year for one person in design; equivalent AI-delivered services (SEO/GEO retainers, ad creative, bookkeeping clean-up) have no verified solo evidence found here. Grant-writing success fees are barred by professional ethics codes.

### Cited Findings
- Designjoy (Brett Williams): one-person productized design service, over $130k MRR, clients pay up to $5k/month, no client calls [SR] — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/i-make-1m-yearly-solopreneur-productised-service-never-have-calls-clients-1728621); [Indie Bites podcast](https://share.transistor.fm/s/8b1f15a3)
- Designjoy annual revenue figures conflict across sources, from ~$1.3M to ~$3.1M (Latka-attributed) [SR/SEC, unverified] — [Starter Story summary](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-make-1-3m-year-with-one-skill); [startups.fm](https://startups.fm/startups/designjoy)
- Designjoy took 4 months at ~$800/month and ran 3.5 years as a side project before scaling; hands-off approach causes some churn [SR] — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-brett-of-designjoy-hit-50k-mrr-plus-a-recap-of-the-latest-milestones-dc21471237)
- Freelance medical writing rates: $50-80/h entry to $250-400+/h for specialised consultants; another guide $75-200/h [SEC, aggregators] — [Jobbers.io](https://www.jobbers.io/medical-writing-freelancing-pharma-healthcare-regulatory-content-2026/); [Health Writer Hub](https://www.healthwriterhub.com/freelance-rates/)
- Grant writing: GPA and AFP ethics codes prohibit percentage/contingent compensation and finder's fees; funders rarely allow the writer's fee in the grant budget, and percentage fees can lead to denial or termination of funding [SEC] — [Nonprofit Marketing Guide](https://www.nonprofitmarketingguide.com/avoiding-unethical-pay-structures-a-guide-for-grant-writers-nonprofit-professionals/); [GrantWatch](https://www.grantwatch.com/grantnews/can-grant-funds-be-used-to-pay-a-grant-writer/)
- One source claims federal law (2 CFR 200.209) also prohibits contingency pay; regulation text not checked [SEC, unverified] — [North Texas Nonprofit Resources](https://northtexasnonprofitresources.org/blog/200)

### Inferences
- Designjoy's outcome rested on personal brand and a large social audience, plus elite craft; the model (fixed monthly price, async delivery, Stripe checkout) is replicable, the distribution is not.
- A grant/RFP service must be flat-fee; "success-fee only" positioning is not viable in the US nonprofit market.

### Gaps
- No verified small-operator evidence for AI SEO/GEO retainers, ad-creative factories, podcast repurposing, bookkeeping clean-up, patent/trademark monitoring, per-report market research, or catalog enrichment. No per-word rates for ISO 17100 regulated translation were found. No AMWA/EMWA survey figures were retrieved (an EMWA freelance survey exists: [EMWA journal](https://journal.emwa.org/freelancing/seventh-freelance-business-survey-the-freelance-landscape-2018-2023)).

## Buying vs building

### Takeaway
Small profitable SaaS trades at roughly 2-3.5x annual profit, so a $50-200k purchase can buy $1.5-5k/month of existing profit on day one; that is a better documented starting point than the median new build, but closed-deal and post-acquisition survival data are missing. AI-enabled roll-ups exist only at institutional scale.

### Cited Findings
- Analysis of 615 Acquire.com listings (2026): median asking price $190,000, 25th percentile $75,000; median ~2.0x trailing revenue and 3.4x trailing profit; averages 2.6x revenue, 10.7x profit (asking, not closed) [SEC] — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/state-of-saas-acquisitions-2026)

### Inferences
- At 2.24x-3.4x profit, a $60k purchase corresponds to roughly $1.5-2.2k/month profit; reaching $5k+/month by acquisition alone needs ~$150-200k or post-purchase growth. Seller financing is available in only about one deal in eight, so capital is the binding constraint.
- An established Shopify or Atlassian app is a logical target because age and reviews drive marketplace ranking.

### Gaps

## Consumer subscription mobile apps

### Takeaway
Not realistic as a hands-off solo route to the target: one year after launch the average app earns about $72/month, and fewer than 5% of new apps reach $10k MRR within two years.

### Cited Findings (all from the RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, released 12 March 2026, 115,000+ apps, $16bn+ revenue, as reported by PPC Land) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)
- One year after launch: average monthly revenue ~$72; top-quartile threshold $429/month; top 10% $2,500+/month [SEC of VP report]
- Share of new apps reaching $1,000 MRR within two years fell from 19% to 17%; share reaching $10,000 MRR fell from 5.3% to 4.6% [SEC]
- Top 25% of apps grew MRR 80%+ year on year; top 10% grew 306%; bottom 25% shrank 33% [SEC]
- 75th-percentile monthly revenue one year after launch by category: Business $4,554; Education $3,614; Productivity $1,250; Travel $822; Gaming $552 [SEC]
- Hard paywall converts 11% vs freemium 2%; revenue per install in two weeks $2.32 vs $0.27 [SEC]
- Median realised LTV per payer after year one: North America $32, Western Europe $25, global $23, South/Southeast Asia $14 [SEC]
- Top 5% of apps earn roughly 400x the bottom 25%, up from 200x in 2024 [VP] — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)
- AI apps earn 41% more per user but churn 30% faster (headline only, not opened) [SEC] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)

### Inferences
- If a mobile app is attempted, Business and Education categories with a hard paywall and North American users have the best unit economics; the model depends on Apple/Google merchant accounts.

### Gaps
- Paid user-acquisition dependence and current Apple/Google commission rates were not sourced in this round.

## Where deep pharma / life-science expertise commands the highest prices

### Takeaway
The highest and most immediate hourly monetisation is expert-network consulting, which needs no capital, no payment rails beyond a bank account and no selling; productized forms (CI reports, tender intelligence) have no public pricing or small-vendor evidence. All routes carry employer-conflict and confidentiality risk.

### Cited Findings
- Client-side prices: GLG $1,000-2,500/hour, AlphaSights $1,500-3,000/hour, Guidepoint $1,000-2,500/hour; annual subscription minimums $50K-$500K (vendor-authored, self-interested estimate) [SEC] — [CleverX](https://cleverx.com/blog/best-expert-network-platforms-for-b2b-research-in-2026/)
- Expert-side: initial rates often pitched at $100-230/hour (older forum data); an anecdote of the same project at $500/hour via GLG vs $400/hour via AlphaSights; GLG known for negotiating expert rates down [SEC, anecdotal] — [Expert Opportunities](https://expertopportunities.com/expert-network-hourly-rates/); [Inex One forum](https://forum.inex.one/-16/how-to-choose-your-expert-network-consulting-rate)
- Incumbent pharma CI platforms (Citeline, GlobalData) price by quote; GlobalData sells single reports through a store with enquiry-based pricing [VP pages, no prices] — [GlobalData store](https://www.globaldata.com/store/report/State-of-Biopharmaceutical-Industry-analysis/); [Citeline on Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/citeline)
- Specialised medical/regulatory writing consultants: $250-400+/hour at the top tier [SEC] — [Jobbers.io](https://www.jobbers.io/medical-writing-freelancing-pharma-healthcare-regulatory-content-2026/)

### Inferences
- Expert-network work is demand-limited (calls arrive irregularly) and personal, so it is a high-margin supplement rather than a scalable business; at an assumed $200-400/hour, 15-25 hours a month would be needed for $5k, which few experts obtain consistently. This volume assumption is not sourced.
- Expert networks screen for conflicts and generally bar discussion of a current employer's confidential information; an employed pharmacist/pharma professional must check employment contract and employer policy first. This is general industry practice stated from background knowledge, not sourced in this round.
- A niche CI or tender-intelligence product for generics exporters is the natural overlap with the earlier-round shortlist (tender alerts, regulatory watch); the absence of public pricing means willingness to pay must be tested directly.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 pharma-specific expert rate card; no evidence on pharma tender-intelligence vendors, API-sourcing intelligence, CMC/quality consulting marketplaces, or regulatory-submission tooling by small vendors.
