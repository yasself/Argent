# Morocco compliance, tax, payments and foreign-exchange framework for a salaried resident running online digital businesses (state of rules: November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-11. Reliability legend used below: **[OFFICIAL]** = read in an official text this session (IGOC 2026 PDF, Law 114-13 on Adala, CNDP sheet, Stripe/Paddle/Lemon Squeezy own pages); **[PRESS]** = Moroccan press reporting an official document; **[FIRM]** = accounting/law-firm or vendor guide, not official; **[UNVERIFIED]** = could not be confirmed, needs an accountant/lawyer. Article numbers of the IGOC 2026 are quoted as cited inside the official text itself or by Upsilon Consulting (the PDF text extraction drops article headings), so article numbers should be re-checked against the PDF before being quoted in a final report.

## 1. Legal statuses (auto-entrepreneur, SARL/SARL AU, personne physique, incentives, Finance Laws 2025/2026, cost comparison)

### Takeaway
The auto-entrepreneur (AE) status is by far the cheapest vehicle up to 200,000 MAD/yr of services (1% final tax on cash received), but it is capped, is hit by a 30% withholding above 80,000 MAD/yr billed to one same client, and its compatibility with a salaried job is not stated in the law itself (commercial guides contradict each other). Above the ceiling a SARL/SARL AU is the realistic vehicle: IS 20% (profit under 100M MAD), 0.25% minimum contribution (3,000 MAD floor), then dividend withholding of 11.25% in 2026 per firm sources (10% from 2027) — a combined ~29% on distributed profit.

### Cited Findings
**Auto-entrepreneur**
- [OFFICIAL] Law 114-13, art. 1: an auto-entrepreneur is "toute personne physique exerçant, à titre individuel, une activité industrielle, commerciale ou artisanale, ou prestataire de services" whose annual cash turnover does not exceed 500,000 MAD (industrial/commercial/artisanal) or 200,000 MAD (services); the activity lists are set by regulation. The text of the law contains no exclusion of salaried employees. — [Loi 114-13, Adala (Ministère de la Justice)](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2024/05/07/Loi%20Relative%20au%20statut%20de%20l%E2%80%99auto-entrepreneur-1715071318209.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] Law 114-13 art. 2: exemption from bookkeeping under Law 9-88 and from registration in the registre du commerce; art. 3: activity may be domiciled at home; art. 8: removal from the register for nil/undeclared turnover during a calendar year (excluding registration year), transformation into a company, non-payment of tax and social contribution for a calendar year, or turnover above the ceilings for two consecutive years. — [Loi 114-13, Adala](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2024/05/07/Loi%20Relative%20au%20statut%20de%20l%E2%80%99auto-entrepreneur-1715071318209.pdf)
- [PRESS, reporting the DGI's 2026 guide; article dated 19 Aug 2026] Rates: 0.5% of cash turnover for commerce/industry/crafts (≤500,000 MAD), 1% for services (≤200,000 MAD), final (libératoire). Service providers: revenue above 80,000 MAD/yr from one same client is subject to a 30% withholding at source by that client. AE is outside the scope of VAT. Five-year exemption from taxe professionnelle. Declarations monthly or quarterly, due by end of the following month. Exceeding the ceiling once does not end the status; two consecutive years does, then move to RNR, or option for RNS/CPU. Excluded professions include architects, lawyers, doctors, dentists, chartered accountants, notaries, pharmacists, veterinarians, publishers, booksellers, etc. Adherence to the social security regime is a condition of eligibility. — [Le Matin, 19/08/2026](https://lematin.ma/economie/auto-entrepreneur-ce-que-prevoit-le-regime-fiscal-en-2026/361537)
- [FIRM] The 30% withholding is based on art. 73-II-G-8° CGI (Finance Law 2023); example: 150,000 MAD billed to one client = 800 MAD (1% on first 80,000) + 21,000 MAD (30% on 70,000). — [ClicPaie guide, updated 29/03/2026](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/auto-entrepreneur-maroc/)
- [FIRM] Registration: online at rn.ae.gov.ma then validation at a Barid Al-Maghrib branch (CIN, photo, proof of address); registration free, about 50 MAD stamp; ICE/IF/TP issued automatically. Export of services is allowed for an AE; import/export of goods is not (decree 2-15-942 exclusion list, 40+ professions). — [ClicPaie guide](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/auto-entrepreneur-maroc/)
- [FIRM, conflicting] Salaried employee + AE: ClicPaie says the texts do not explicitly prohibit it, provided the AE activity differs from the job, the employment contract has no exclusivity clause and the employee does not compete with the employer — [ClicPaie](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/auto-entrepreneur-maroc/); contradicted by another commercial guide stating the AE status is incompatible with an employment contract (CDI/CDD) and civil-servant status, without citing an article — [Humantal guide](https://humantal.ma/ressources/statut-auto-entrepreneur-maroc-guide-complet). The law's art. 1 (above) contains no such incompatibility.
- [FIRM, low confidence] ClicPaie reproduces a quarterly CNSS/AMO contribution table for AEs ranging from 300 MAD/quarter (minimum, due even with nil turnover) to 3,600 MAD/quarter, and a 3-month waiting period for AMO; the table's bracket labels look garbled, and the page itself recommends checking cnss.ma. — [ClicPaie](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/auto-entrepreneur-maroc/)

**SARL / SARL AU and corporate tax**
- [FIRM] IS: convergence to target rates completed over 2023-2026: 20% for net taxable profit under 100M MAD, 35% at/above 100M MAD. — [Upsilon, IS barème 2025-2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/calcul-impot-societes-bareme-progressif/); [Upsilon LF 2026 page (reviewed 05/11/2026)](https://upsilon-consulting.com/loi-de-finances-2026-au-maroc/)
- [FIRM] Cotisation minimale: 0.25% of operating + financial income + subsidies, minimum 3,000 MAD, due even in a loss year, except during the first 36 months of activity. — [ClicPaie IS guide, updated 06/04/2026](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/impot-societes-maroc/). A search-engine summary of press mentioned "recul du taux normal à 0,15%" — not confirmed in any page read; treat as unverified. — [Aujourd'hui le Maroc, LF 2026 measures](https://aujourdhui.ma/economie/tout-savoir-sur-les-mesures-fiscales-applicables-a-partir-du-1er-janvier-2026)
- [FIRM, internally inconsistent] Dividend withholding schedule: 13.75% (2023), 12.5% (2024 and 2025), 11.25% (2026), 10% (2027); the rate is that of the year of distribution. The page itself contains an inconsistency on 2024, so the 2026 rate (11.25% vs 10%) must be confirmed in CGI 2026 / an accountant. — [Upsilon, RAS dividendes (reviewed 05/11/2026)](https://upsilon-consulting.com/taux-de-retenue-a-la-source-sur-dividendes/)
- [FIRM] SARL: no legal minimum capital (1 MAD possible; 10,000 MAD recommended for credibility); DIY administrative creation cost 1,500–2,300 MAD vs 3,660–6,110 MAD through a provider; another firm quotes 5,000–15,000 MAD excluding capital; greffe ~350 MAD; negative certificate ~230 MAD; domiciliation 300–800 MAD/month; annual running costs (accounting, CNSS, patente, domiciliation) estimated 15,000–50,000 MAD; delay 7–30 days. — [ClicPaie, créer SARL (30/03/2026)](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/creer-sarl-maroc/); [Upsilon, coût création SARL 2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/cout-creation-sarl-maroc-2026/)
- [OFFICIAL] Under Law 114-13 art. 8, turning the activity into a company of any form triggers removal from the AE register (so AE and the same activity in a company do not coexist). — [Loi 114-13](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2024/05/07/Loi%20Relative%20au%20statut%20de%20l%E2%80%99auto-entrepreneur-1715071318209.pdf)

**Finance Law 2026 (Law 50-25, dahir 10/12/2025, BO 16/12/2025) and DGI circular 737**
- [FIRM] Main LF 2026 measures: Contribution sociale de solidarité extended 2026-2028 for profits ≥1M MAD (1.5% for 1–5M, 2.5% for 5–10M, 3.5% for 10–40M, 5% above 40M) — also applies to individuals under the real regime with net professional income ≥1M MAD; 5% withholding on rents from 1 July 2026; VAT withholding at 75%/100% on service fees paid by very large payers (turnover thresholds 500M MAD from 01/07/2026, 350M from 2027, 200M from 2028); CFC employees' flat 20% IR for up to 10 years; additional 2% registration duty on property/business transfers not paid by traceable means. Nothing in the Deloitte summary changes the AE regime, general IS rates, or creates a new startup/JEI tax regime. — [Deloitte Société d'Avocats, 07/04/2026](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/maroc-les-principales-mesures-de-la-loi-de-finances-pour-2026/)
- [FIRM] IR: family-charge deduction raised from 500 to 600 MAD per dependant (cap 3,600 MAD) from 1 Jan 2026; the IR scale was revised in 2025. — [Upsilon LF 2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/loi-de-finances-2026-au-maroc/)
- [PRESS] DGI published circular note n°737 on LF 2026 tax measures. — [La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/argent/budget/document-la-dgi-publie-la-note-circulaire-de-la-loi-de-finances-2026/); copy: [note circulaire 737 PDF (hosted by Upsilon)](https://upsilon-consulting.com/documents/note-circulaire-737-ldf-2026.pdf)

**Startup / JEI label, exporter and CFC**
- [OFFICIAL] The "jeunes entreprises innovantes en nouvelles technologies labellisées par l'ADD" label is a foreign-exchange facility (not a tax regime) in the IGOC 2026: 2,000,000 MAD/yr e-commerce allowance and up to 10,000,000 MAD/yr of investment abroad without the 3-year-activity and statutory-auditor conditions. — [IGOC 2026, Office des Changes](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- [PRESS] The label is granted by the Agence de Développement du Digital via the Startup Hub portal (startuphubmaroc.ma), with Office des Changes, OMPIC, CCG and CGEM associated. — [Finances News Hebdo](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/lancement-de-la-plateforme-digitale-startuphubmaroc-ma)
- [OFFICIAL] CFC-status companies holding foreign-currency accounts may pay for services related to their activity up to the balance of those accounts. — [IGOC 2026](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- [PRESS, title only] The former export-specific IS incentives were removed in earlier reforms ("Exportations: les incitations fiscales sont passées à la trappe"). — [Challenge.ma](https://www.challenge.ma/exportations-les-incitations-fiscales-sont-passees-a-la-trappe-302982/)

### Inferences
Illustrative comparison (my own arithmetic from the rates above; assumptions: services activity, no single Moroccan client above 80,000 MAD, SARL AU with 20,000 MAD/yr structure costs (accounting, domiciliation — mid-low of the 15,000–50,000 firm estimate), no manager salary, full distribution, dividend withholding 11.25%, IS 20%, minimum contribution ignored when lower than IS; AE social contributions excluded because the amounts are unverified; other business expenses ignored in both cases):

| Annual revenue | AE (services, 1%) | SARL AU (IS 20% + dividend WHT 11.25%) |
|---|---|---|
| 50,000 MAD | Tax 500 → ~49,500 net (before CNSS) | Profit 30,000 → IS 6,000 → WHT 2,700 → ~21,300 net (43% of revenue) |
| 200,000 MAD | Tax 2,000 → ~198,000 net (before CNSS) | Profit 180,000 → IS 36,000 → WHT 16,200 → ~127,800 net (64%) |
| 600,000 MAD | Not available: above the 200,000 services ceiling (and above the 500,000 commerce ceiling) | Profit 580,000 → IS 116,000 → WHT 52,200 → ~411,800 net (69%) |

- Combined IS + dividend load on distributed profit is 1 − 0.80 × 0.8875 ≈ 29% in 2026 (28% if/when the dividend rate is 10%).
- The AE regime is ~20–30x cheaper in tax than a company at 50k–200k MAD, but only works if clients are diversified: an AE earning 200,000 MAD from a single Moroccan client would bear 800 + 30% × 120,000 = 36,800 MAD.
- A SARL has real expenses deductible (API costs, ads, hosting), which AE does not (tax on gross receipts): for low-margin activities (e.g. heavy API/ads spend) the AE advantage shrinks, but at 1% of turnover it remains ahead in almost any realistic margin scenario below the ceiling.
- Because AE tax is final, AE income should not stack on the salary in the progressive IR scale; an individual business under the real regime (personne physique RNR) would stack on salary at marginal rates up to the top bracket — to be confirmed by an accountant.
- Whether "digital products / SaaS subscriptions" count as services (200,000 ceiling, 1%) or commerce (500,000 ceiling, 0.5%) is a classification question; prudently assume services.

### Gaps
- No official DGI/CGI 2026 text was read directly for: IS brackets, cotisation minimale, the 2026 dividend withholding rate (11.25% vs 10%), the 2025 IR scale brackets, the AE 30% rule's application when the client is a non-resident (no Moroccan withholding agent). All need accountant validation against CGI 2026 and circular 737.
- AE social contributions (CNSS/AMO amounts, "droits complémentaires") for 2026: no reliable official figures found; for an employee already covered by CNSS through the employer, how the AE contribution interacts is unverified.
- Eligibility of a salaried employee for AE: the law does not exclude it, but no official ae.gov.ma / DGI statement was retrieved; commercial guides conflict. Ask Barid Al-Maghrib/Maroc PME or a lawyer.
- No evidence found of a tax "statut de l'exportateur de services" or a "jeune entreprise innovante" tax regime in 2026; CFC status requirements (it targets financial/regional HQ/service companies with substance) were not researched in depth and it is unlikely to be relevant to a solo business.
- Personne physique regimes (CPU, RNS, RNR) thresholds and rates in 2026 not verified.

## 2. VAT (export of services, Moroccan consumers, EU OSS, merchant-of-record, US sales tax)

### Takeaway
Exported services are VAT-exempt with right of deduction (art. 92-I-1° CGI) if the client is abroad and the service is used abroad; an AE is outside VAT altogether. For EU/US consumer sales, a merchant-of-record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, etc.) becomes the seller and handles EU VAT/US sales tax, which is the practical route for a Moroccan micro-business; Morocco itself now taxes foreign digital providers selling to Moroccan consumers (art. 115 bis CGI, platform live since June 2026).

### Cited Findings
- [FIRM] Export of services: exemption with right of deduction under art. 92-I-1° CGI; three cumulative conditions per art. 88 and circular 717: client established outside Morocco, contract denominated in foreign currency, service used/exploited outside Morocco (decisive criterion); proof: foreign-currency contract, invoices to the foreign client, bank credit advices showing repatriation, documents proving use abroad; invoice without VAT and mention "Exonération de TVA — Article 92-I-1° du CGI". If a condition fails, 20% (or 10%) applies. — [Upsilon, TVA et export de services 2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/tva-export-services-maroc/)
- [PRESS, reporting DGI guide] The auto-entrepreneur is outside the scope of VAT (turnover below the 500,000 MAD threshold). — [Le Matin, 19/08/2026](https://lematin.ma/economie/auto-entrepreneur-ce-que-prevoit-le-regime-fiscal-en-2026/361537)
- [PRESS] Foreign digital providers: art. 115 bis CGI (Finance Law 2024) makes non-resident suppliers of remote digital services to Moroccan customers not subject to VAT (consumers) register, declare turnover and remit Moroccan VAT via the SIMPL "Taxation des services numériques" platform; the implementing decree was published in the BO in December 2025; formalities applicable from 11 June 2026; quarterly filing; services covered include streaming, SaaS, cloud, mobile apps, AI tools, digital advertising, online software and training. — [Hespress, 23/05/2026](https://fr.hespress.com/473798-tva-la-dgi-deploie-son-dispositif-visant-les-services-digitaux-etrangers.html); decree text: [décret art. 115 bis (PDF hosted by EcoActu)](https://ecoactu.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/decret-pour-la-TVA-sur-les-services-a-distance-au-Maroc-fournis-par-des-prestataires-non-residents-Art-115-Bis-du-CGI.pdf). Sources conflict on filing frequency (Hespress: quarterly; Fiscamaroc: monthly) and on whether a fiscal representative is required. — [Fiscamaroc art. 115 bis](https://www.fiscamaroc.com/la-taxe-valeur-ajoutee-126/article-115-bis-obligations-des-fournisseurs-de-services-a-distance-non-residents-1053.htm)
- [FIRM] When a foreign provider supplies a service used in Morocco to a Moroccan business, Moroccan VAT is due via reverse charge/withholding (art. 115 CGI); digital services rate 20%. — [Upsilon, TVA services numériques 2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/tva-services-numeriques-maroc/)
- [OFFICIAL, vendor] Lemon Squeezy (merchant of record) lists Morocco among countries supported for bank payouts; PayPal payouts in 200+ countries. — [Lemon Squeezy docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries)
- [OFFICIAL, vendor] Paddle's list of unsupported seller countries (28 entries: Afghanistan, Belarus, Cuba, Iran, Libya, Mali, Russia, Syria, etc.) does not include Morocco. — [Paddle help](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/account-verification/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle)

### Inferences
- Practical consequence for a Moroccan SARL selling digital services to Moroccan consumers: standard 20% VAT applies once the seller is a VAT taxpayer; an AE does not charge VAT. This means the Moroccan B2C price of a SARL is at a 20% disadvantage vs. an AE, but at par with foreign platforms now subject to art. 115 bis.
- Background rules not re-fetched this session (stable, but cite primary sources before publishing): (a) EU: B2C digital services are taxed where the consumer resides, with no threshold for non-EU sellers; a non-EU seller selling directly must register for the non-Union OSS scheme in one Member State and file quarterly ([EU VAT One Stop Shop portal](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/index_en)); B2B sales to EU VAT-registered businesses are generally reverse-charged by the customer. (b) Merchant-of-record platforms (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, FastSpring, Gumroad since 2025) are legally the seller to the end customer and collect/remit EU VAT and US sales tax; the Moroccan business then makes a B2B export sale to the platform (a non-resident), which fits the export-of-services VAT exemption and the Office des Changes repatriation logic. (c) US: no federal VAT; state sales-tax "economic nexus" thresholds after South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018) typically ~100,000 USD of sales per state; many states tax SaaS/digital goods; a MoR removes that burden.
- Using a MoR also gives a single foreign payer, a clean contract and SWIFT/bank payouts — the documents the bank and the Office des Changes expect for export of services.

### Gaps
- CGI 2026 articles 88, 91, 92, 115 bis not read in the official DGI text; VAT registration threshold and VAT treatment for a company selling both to Morocco and abroad need an accountant.
- EU OSS and US sales-tax statements above come from background knowledge, not from pages fetched this session.
- Fee schedules of Paddle (5% + 0.50 USD historically), Lemon Squeezy, Gumroad, FastSpring were not verified this session; Gumroad and FastSpring availability/payout methods for Morocco not verified (Gumroad says it pays by PayPal where bank payouts are unsupported — [Gumroad help](https://gumroad.com/help/article/13-getting-paid)).

## 3. Payment providers actually available to Moroccan residents/companies in 2026

### Takeaway
Stripe still does not onboard Morocco (not in its list of 51 markets, Nov 2026). The workable legal routes are: merchant-of-record platforms paying out to a Moroccan bank (Lemon Squeezy confirmed; Paddle does not exclude Morocco), PayPal with withdrawal to a Moroccan bank or CashPlus, Payoneer, direct SWIFT transfers, and for the domestic market CMI/acquirers and aggregators (Payzone, YouCan Pay). A US LLC/Stripe Atlas structure is a foreign asset/account requiring Office des Changes authorization for a Moroccan resident.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, vendor] Stripe's global availability page lists 51 countries/regions; Morocco is not listed; African markets shown are Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa ("extended network"), plus UAE in the Middle East. — [stripe.com/global (fetched 11/11/2026)](https://stripe.com/global)
- [OFFICIAL] IGOC 2026 general provisions: any operation not covered by the Instruction requires Office des Changes authorization, expressly including "l'ouverture de comptes à l'étranger par les personnes marocaines résidentes" and "la constitution d'avoirs à l'étranger par les personnes marocaines résidentes"; foreign assets are defined as financial instruments, real estate, foreign-currency liquidity and "tout bien, droit et intérêt" held outside Morocco. Investment abroad under the general regime is reserved to Moroccan legal entities registered in the RC with at least 3 years of activity and accounts certified without reservation by a statutory auditor, related to their activity. — [IGOC 2026](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL, vendor] Lemon Squeezy: Morocco is on the bank-payout country list. — [Lemon Squeezy docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries)

### Inferences
- Stripe Atlas / US LLC: forming a foreign company and holding its US bank/Stripe balances is, for a Moroccan resident individual, both a "constitution d'avoirs à l'étranger" and an "ouverture de compte à l'étranger" — operations the IGOC subjects to Office des Changes authorization; the general outbound-investment regime is for 3-year-old audited Moroccan companies, with a derogation for ADD-labelled startups (10M MAD/yr). So the Atlas route is not legally available to an individual employee/AE without a specific authorization; the compliant sequence would be Moroccan company → ADD label (or 3 years + audit) → declared investment abroad. Needs a lawyer; do not treat as a workaround.
- Payment-platform balances (PayPal, Payoneer) are foreign-currency liquidity held abroad: the safe practice is prompt withdrawal to a Moroccan bank within the 90-day repatriation window (PayPal's own 30-day rule is stricter), keeping invoices/contracts for the bank.
- Receiving crypto as payment remains legally risky/unsupported (no law in force, foreign-exchange rules require repatriation through banks); exclude from a compliance-first plan.
- Ranking for a compliance-first solo seller: (1) MoR (Lemon Squeezy/Paddle) with bank payout to Morocco; (2) direct SWIFT/SEPA invoices for B2B clients; (3) PayPal for small ad-hoc payments; (4) Payoneer mainly for marketplaces; domestic: Payzone/YouCan Pay/CMI acquirers.

### Gaps
- Not researched or not verifiable this session: 2Checkout/Verifone, FastSpring, Gumroad (Morocco payout specifics), Chari, PayDunya, CinetPay, Flutterwave, Paystack (whether they onboard Moroccan merchants), exact Payzone/YouCan Pay tariffs, KYC documents per provider, Paddle payout methods/currencies to Morocco, official PayPal Morocco fee page and receiving limits.
- Whether an AE (not in the registre du commerce) passes KYC as a "business" at Paddle/Lemon Squeezy is unverified.
- Final status of the crypto law after April 2026 not found; BAM/Office des Changes' 2017 warnings against crypto transactions were not re-sourced this session.

## 4. Foreign-exchange regulation (Office des Changes, IGOC 2026)

### Takeaway
The IGOC 2026 (in force 1 January 2026) obliges service exporters to repatriate 100% of proceeds within 90 days of performing the service, lets them keep up to 70% in foreign-currency/convertible-dirham accounts, and raises e-commerce allowances: 20,000 MAD/yr for individuals, a 50,000 MAD/yr floor for new/low-tax companies, up to 200,000 MAD for ordinary companies, 2,000,000 MAD for ADD-labelled startups. Opening accounts or building assets abroad without authorization remains prohibited for Moroccan residents; the last amnesty closed on 31 December 2024.

### Cited Findings (all [OFFICIAL] from the IGOC 2026 PDF unless stated)
- IGOC 2026 published by the Office des Changes, effective 1 January 2026. — [Office des Changes announcement](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/actualites/publication-de-l-instruction-generale-des-operations-de-change-2026); full text: [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- Export of services definition: "prestations rendues au Maroc ou à l'étranger par un résident en faveur d'un non-résident et donnant lieu à une rémunération"; must be covered by a service contract. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf) (art. 95-96 per [Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/en/export-goods-services-exchange-morocco/))
- Repatriation: exporters of services must repatriate "le montant intégral des recettes" within "quatre-vingt-dix (90) jours à compter de la date de la réalisation des prestations de services"; in case of non-payment the exporter must pursue recovery and keep the Office informed. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf) (art. 97). (Goods: 150 days per [Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/en/export-goods-services-exchange-morocco/); the 60-day figure on an older oc.gov.ma page refers to the 2013 instruction.)
- Exporter accounts: banks may open foreign-currency or convertible-dirham accounts for service exporters "personnes morales ou physiques inscrites au registre du commerce"; credit limited to 70% of repatriated export receipts (85% for categorized operators and aerospace); balance must be sold on the FX market; funds serve professional foreign-currency expenses, including by international card. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf) (art. 98, 229)
- Individuals not in the registre du commerce: banks may open foreign-currency/convertible-dirham accounts for "personnes physiques marocaines résidentes, non inscrites au registre de commerce, disposant de revenus de source étrangère", declared in writing to the bank with the nature of the operation; credit up to 70% of repatriated foreign-source income, remainder sold; debits allowed for any current-operation payment abroad, excluding purchase of real estate, financial assets or any constitution of assets abroad. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf) (art. 234)
- Permitted inbound payment channels: bank transfer received from abroad, debit of convertible/foreign-currency accounts, international postal money orders; other modes (foreign banknotes, international payment cards) under the Instruction's conditions. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- E-commerce definition: any online purchase paid by international payment card by (a) resident individuals and MREs under their e-commerce allowance; (b) ADD-labelled young innovative tech companies for services directly linked to their activity; (c) Moroccan-law entities without foreign-currency accounts for imported services; (d) holders of foreign-currency/convertible-dirham accounts up to their balances (exporters, individuals with foreign-source income, CFC companies, etc.). — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf) (art. 120)
- E-commerce allowance amounts per calendar year: 20,000 MAD per resident individual/MRE (raised from 15,000); 2,000,000 MAD for ADD-labelled startups (raised from 1,000,000); up to 1,000,000 MAD for categorized operators (100% of IS/IR paid for last closed year); up to 200,000 MAD for Moroccan-law entities without foreign-currency accounts, based on 100% of IS, IR or minimum contribution paid for the last closed year; 50,000 MAD for Moroccan companies without such accounts whose IS/IR paid is below 50,000 MAD, tax-exempt companies and newly created companies; up to available balances for holders of foreign-currency accounts. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf); press confirmation: [EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/igoc-dotations-ecommerce-plafonds-instruments-2026/)
- Card issuance documents: CIN for individuals; ADD label certificate for startups; tax payment receipt for the last closed year for companies; RC extract for newly created companies. Unused allowances at 31 December are not carried over; one nominative international card can carry all allowances. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- Import of services (paying foreign suppliers by transfer): defined as services rendered to a resident by a non-resident for remuneration; "services informatiques et d'information", marketing/advertising targeting an audience abroad, training and telecom services may be rendered abroad; IGOC 2026 removed the former limitative list delegated to banks; eligible importers are "personnes morales ou physiques inscrites au registre du commerce et disposant d'un identifiant fiscal" (plus public bodies, cooperatives, etc.); a contract is required; the importer is responsible for effectiveness and market price. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf) (art. 69 ff.)
- Foreign assets: individuals who "entrent en possession d'avoirs ou de liquidités à l'étranger" must declare them to the Office des Changes within 3 months (annex 3 form). Accounts abroad for service exporters are allowed only for holders/bidders of contracts won through tenders abroad, must be closed at completion and balances repatriated without delay. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- JEI derogation: founders of ADD-labelled startups with a firm financing commitment from foreign funders may create foreign-law entities by contributing their shares; the labelled startup may invest abroad up to 10,000,000 MAD per year without the 3-year and auditor conditions; income and disposal proceeds must be repatriated within 30 days. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- Travel: business-travel allowance for companies without foreign-currency accounts raised to 1,000,000 MAD (100% of prior-year tax); personal travel base 100,000 MAD plus supplement of 30% of IR up to 400,000 MAD, total capped at 500,000 MAD per person per year. — [IGOC 2026 PDF](http://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/2026-01/IGOC%202026_1.pdf)
- [PRESS/OFFICIAL] Amnesty: the third "opération de régularisation spontanée" (art. 8 of Finance Law 55-23 for 2024) ran from 1 January to 31 December 2024 for assets held abroad before 30 September 2023, against a liberatory contribution; reported result 658 declarations for more than 2 billion MAD. It is closed. — [Office des Changes](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/actualites/operation-regularisation-spontanee-2024); [TelQuel, 03/01/2024](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2024/01/03/office-des-changes-operation-de-regularisation-spontanee-du-1er-janvier-au-31-decembre-2024_1849143/); [H24info](https://h24info.ma/?p=879375)
- [PRESS] Enforcement signals: the Office des Changes has audited influencers' undeclared service exports and requires remuneration to be repatriated within 90 days; in May 2026 it questioned exporters suspected of hiding profits in accounts abroad. — [Hespress, influenceurs](https://fr.hespress.com/396583-396583.html); [Finances News Hebdo, 90 jours](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/office-des-changes-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours); [Hespress, May 2026](https://fr.hespress.com/?p=472476)

### Inferences
- The words "auto-entrepreneur" never appear in the IGOC 2026 (full-text search of the official PDF). An AE is a natural person not registered in the registre du commerce (Law 114-13 art. 2), so: (i) for paying foreign SaaS/API/ads by card, the AE falls back on the individual allowance of 20,000 MAD/yr (≈ 2,000 USD) unless they have foreign-source income, in which case the art. 234 account lets them keep up to 70% of repatriated foreign income in foreign currency and spend it abroad by card with no fixed ceiling; (ii) the "import of services" regime by bank transfer and the exporter account (art. 98) are worded for persons "inscrites au registre du commerce", so they appear closed to an AE. This is the single most important structural constraint for an AI/API-heavy business run as AE and should be validated with the bank's trade desk.
- A newly created SARL/SARL AU gets at least 50,000 MAD/yr of card e-commerce allowance from day one (RC extract), rising to 100% of tax paid up to 200,000 MAD, plus access to the import-of-services regime by transfer (contract/invoice based, no fixed ceiling stated) and, as an exporter, the 70% foreign-currency account. A company therefore solves the "how do I pay Anthropic/Google Ads/hosting" problem much better than AE; the ADD startup label lifts the card allowance to 2,000,000 MAD.
- Export revenue first, then spend: for an export-oriented business the cleanest way to fund foreign API/ads bills is the 70% foreign-currency retention, since spending from those accounts is limited only by the balance.
- Art. 98 covers "personnes physiques inscrites au registre du commerce" — a sole trader (personne physique) registered in the RC would qualify where an AE may not.

### Gaps
- Whether banks in practice open art. 234 accounts for AEs receiving MoR/PayPal/Payoneer payouts, and what proof they ask, is unverified (bank-level practice).
- Exact contribution rates of the 2024 amnesty and sanctions for unauthorised foreign accounts/companies (dahir of 30 August 1949 and related texts) were not retrieved.
- Whether card payments to foreign SaaS by a company count against the e-commerce allowance or can instead be settled as "import of services" by transfer without ceiling — practical bank treatment to confirm.
- Article numbering (97, 98, 120, 229, 234) is taken from in-text cross-references and Upsilon; verify in the PDF.

## 5. Data protection, e-commerce/consumer law, e-invoicing, trust services, cold emailing

### Takeaway
Law 09-08 remains in force (no GDPR-style reform adopted): processing must be notified to the CNDP before launch, transfers abroad (including foreign cloud/SaaS/LLM APIs) are regulated, and email/SMS prospecting requires prior consent except for existing customers and analogous products. Serving EU customers adds GDPR extraterritorial obligations; mandatory e-invoicing is legislated (art. 145-IX CGI) but its decree/timeline was still unconfirmed in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] CNDP direct-marketing sheet: prospecting by SMS/email is allowed only with the recipient's prior consent and after declaring the processing to the CNDP; exception — no prior consent needed if the advertiser collected the contact details directly from the recipient during a previous sale of analogous products/services; in all cases a free, easy opt-out must be offered; subcontracted campaigns need a contract and a legitimately built database; art. 59 of Law 09-08: 3 months to 1 year imprisonment and/or 20,000–200,000 MAD fine for processing despite opposition or for electronic prospecting in breach of art. 10. The sheet makes no B2B exception. — [CNDP, fiche Prospection directe (July 2025)](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/fiche_CNDP_Prospection_RT_fr.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] The CNDP published a processing-notification procedure dated 24 July 2025 (file must include a summary of the study when data is transferred abroad). — [CNDP procedure](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/CNDP_Procedure-de-Notification-des-traitements_20250724.pdf)
- [FIRM] Law 09-08: processing must be notified to the CNDP before implementation (art. 12); transfers outside Morocco are governed by arts. 43-44 (destination state must ensure sufficient protection, otherwise CNDP authorization); use of cloud services hosted abroad is an international transfer; criminal sanctions for unauthorized transfer (arts. 60-61). — [Upsilon, CNDP et loi 09-08](https://upsilon-consulting.com/cndp-loi-09-08-protection-donnees-personnelles-maroc/); [Upsilon, transfert international](https://upsilon-consulting.com/transfert-international-donnees-personnelles-maroc/)
- [PRESS] CNDP president Omar Seghrouchni (Dec 2025 interview) frames data protection compliance as a competitive advantage for Morocco; a TelQuel FAQ on data protection was published 10/04/2026; no adopted reform of Law 09-08 surfaced. — [TelQuel, 26/12/2025](https://telquel.ma/2025/12/26/omar-seghrouchni-le-respect-de-la-protection-des-donnees-est-un-avantage-competitif-du-maroc_1967844); [TelQuel FAQ, 10/04/2026](https://telquel.ma/2026/04/10/faq-protection-des-donnees-tout-ce-quil-faut-savoir_1983971)
- [FIRM/PRESS] E-invoicing: legal basis art. 145-IX CGI; centralized model with UBL format, national platform developed under DGI supervision; the precise calendar and thresholds depend on an implementing decree reported as still at the Secrétariat général du gouvernement; one software-vendor blog claims large IS companies from 1 Jan 2026 and mid-size from 1 July 2026, unconfirmed by other sources. — [Upsilon, facturation électronique 2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/facturation-electronique-maroc-2026/); [Le360, calendrier](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/facturation-electronique-le-detail-du-calendrier-de-mise-en-place_2EQSNG2DTVB6PGBBHQ6OCFT6CY/); [Inyad (vendor)](https://inyad.com/blog/calendrier-facture-electronique-maroc-2026-2028)

### Inferences
- Background rules not re-fetched this session (verify against primary texts): GDPR art. 3(2) applies to a Moroccan business offering goods/services to individuals in the EU or monitoring their behaviour; art. 27 then requires an EU representative unless processing is occasional, not large-scale sensitive data and low-risk; Morocco has no EU adequacy decision ([Commission adequacy list](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en)), so EU business customers sending personal data to a Moroccan processor will require SCCs and a DPA; a MoR handling checkout reduces but does not remove the seller's own GDPR role for product/user data.
- Cold emailing: Morocco — opt-in by default with a narrow existing-customer exception and no stated B2B carve-out (CNDP sheet), plus CNDP declaration, so cold emailing Moroccan individuals' addresses is high-risk. France — CNIL doctrine allows B2B email prospecting without prior consent if the message relates to the recipient's professional role, with information and opt-out ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique)); other EU states differ (e.g. Germany requires prior consent in B2B) — country-by-country check needed. US — CAN-SPAM is opt-out: no deceptive headers/subjects, valid physical postal address, working unsubscribe honoured within 10 business days ([FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).
- An AI product sending customer personal data to US LLM APIs is an international transfer under Law 09-08 (CNDP formality) and, for EU data subjects, an onward transfer under GDPR.

### Gaps
- Law 31-08 (consumer protection: distance-selling information duties, 7-day withdrawal right and its digital-content exceptions), Law 53-05 (electronic exchange of legal data) and Law 43-20 (trust services, electronic signatures) were not researched this session — no sourced findings.
- Text of Law 09-08 arts. 10, 12, 43-44 not read directly; CNDP list of countries with adequate protection and simplified regimes not retrieved.
- E-invoicing decree: publication status and whether AEs/small companies are in scope in 2026-2027 not confirmed.
- EU ePrivacy B2B rules country by country not researched.

## 6. Employment side: can a private-sector employee hold AE status or own/manage a company?

### Takeaway
No retrieved text prohibits a private-sector employee from having a side business: Law 114-13 defines the AE without excluding employees, and the Labour Code (Law 65-99) has no general ban, but contractual exclusivity/non-compete clauses and the duty of loyalty can make it a dismissible fault. This area rests mostly on firm guides and comparative reasoning and needs a labour lawyer's review of the actual employment contract.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Law 114-13 art. 1 defines the AE as any natural person exercising individually an eligible activity under the ceilings; no exclusion of salaried persons appears in the law. — [Loi 114-13, Adala](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2024/05/07/Loi%20Relative%20au%20statut%20de%20l%E2%80%99auto-entrepreneur-1715071318209.pdf)
- [FIRM, conflicting] One HR guide: cumul possible if the AE activity is different from the job, no exclusivity clause in the contract, no competition with the employer — [ClicPaie](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/auto-entrepreneur-maroc/); another guide asserts incompatibility with CDI/CDD without legal reference — [Humantal](https://humantal.ma/ressources/statut-auto-entrepreneur-maroc-guide-complet).
- [FIRM] The 30% withholding above 80,000 MAD per client (LF 2023) targets disguised employment (salary relationships dressed up as AE services). — [Upsilon, AE régime fiscal 2026](https://upsilon-consulting.com/auto-entrepreneur-ir-regime-fiscal-maroc/); [Médias24, 23/10/2022](https://medias24.com/2022/10/23/detourne-le-statut-de-lauto-entrepreneur-peut-servir-devasion-fiscale-le-gouvernement-reagit/)
- [FIRM] Non-compete clauses are not provided for by the Moroccan Labour Code; they stem from practice and case law; confidentiality, exclusivity and post-contract non-compete are the usual protective clauses. — [LPA law firm note (2020)](https://www.lpalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/2020.04-_Conjoncture_Lina-Fassi-Fihri.pdf)
- [FIRM] Art. 39 of the Labour Code gives an indicative (non-exhaustive) list of serious misconduct justifying dismissal without notice. — [CMS, licenciement pour faute grave au Maroc](https://cms.law/en/fra/legal-updates/ce-qu-il-faut-savoir-sur-le-licenciement-pour-faute-grave-au-maroc); Labour Code text: [Code du travail, Adala](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2024/04/30/code%20du%20travail-1714463246806.pdf)

### Inferences
- Practical checklist for the employee: read the contract and internal rules for exclusivity, non-compete, IP-assignment and outside-activity declaration clauses; never use employer time, equipment, data or clients; avoid any activity competing with the employer; never invoice one's own employer as AE (disguised-employment risk plus the 30% rule); consider written disclosure/authorization where a clause exists.
- For a pharma-sector employee, a side business in health/pharma information products is the scenario most exposed to conflict-of-interest, confidentiality and loyalty arguments; a clearly unrelated niche is safer.
- Being a shareholder of a SARL is generally less sensitive than being its gérant (management role visible in the RC and time-consuming); no source retrieved on any legal incompatibility for a private employee to be gérant.

### Gaps
- No Moroccan case law or Labour Code article specifically on cumul of salaried work and independent/commercial activity was found; searches mostly returned French-law material.
- No official ae.gov.ma / Maroc PME FAQ on employee eligibility retrieved.
- CNSS consequences (double affiliation, AMO) for an employee who is also AE or gérant not verified.

## 7. Sector constraints for pharma/health information products

### Takeaway
Law 17-04 confines the sale of medicines to pharmacies, bans online sale and unauthorised advertising of medicines (public advertising now requires prior authorization from the Moroccan medicines agency), with fines of 5,000–50,000 MAD for advertising breaches; pharmacists are excluded from the AE status. Pure educational/information products are not addressed by the sources found and should avoid drug promotion, individual medical advice and anything resembling dispensing.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS] Advertising of medicines is restricted under Law 17-04; art. 151 punishes breaches of the advertising rules with a fine of 5,000 to 50,000 MAD, doubled on repeat offence. — [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/178064-publicite-de-medicaments-une-pratique-illegale-et-un-danger-de-sante-publique.html)
- [PRESS] New decrees adopted by the Government Council regulate the advertising and sale of medicines: advertising to the general public is subject to prior authorization from the Agence marocaine des médicaments et des produits de santé (arts. 42 and 44 of Law 17-04). — [Finances News Hebdo](https://fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/projets-medicament-gouvernement); [SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/nouveau-cadre-reglementaire-pour-la-publicite-des-medicaments-au-maroc-108785)
- [PRESS] Online sale of medicines is illegal: the Health Minister (Jan 2025) recalled that Law 17-04 prohibits supplying, offering or selling medicines and pharmaceutical products to the public outside the legally prescribed (pharmacy) channel. — [Hespress, 2025](https://fr.hespress.com/404325-achat-en-ligne-de-medicaments-les-mises-en-gardes-du-ministre-tahraoui.html)
- [PRESS] An October 2026 TelQuel article reports a new "Code" taking over the essentials of Law 17-04 for regulating medical representatives, samples/gifts and advertising. — [TelQuel, 12/10/2026](https://telquel.ma/2026/10/12/delegues-medicaux-cadeaux-encadres-influence-ouverte_2012520)
- [PRESS, reporting DGI guide] Pharmacists, doctors, dentists and veterinarians are among professions excluded from the AE regime. — [Le Matin, 19/08/2026](https://lematin.ma/economie/auto-entrepreneur-ce-que-prevoit-le-regime-fiscal-en-2026/361537)

### Inferences
- Safe perimeter for a digital business: B2B tools, training and general educational content with disclaimers; no naming/promotion of prescription medicines to the public, no personalised diagnosis/treatment advice (illegal practice of medicine/pharmacy risk), no intermediation in drug sales. Health data would also be sensitive data under Law 09-08 (CNDP authorization) and GDPR art. 9.
- A practising pharmacist or pharma employee also faces professional-order ethics rules and employer confidentiality on top of the above.

### Gaps
- No source found on the legal status of health-information websites, telemedicine platforms or paid health courses in Morocco; the texts of Law 17-04 (arts. 42-44, 112, 151), Law 131-13 (practice of medicine, telemedicine decree) and the 2026 "Code" were not read directly.
- Rules in target markets (EU directive 2001/83 ban on advertising prescription medicines to the public, US FDA/FTC rules) not researched.
