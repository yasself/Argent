## Q1. Which niches have expensive incumbents (>10k USD/yr) and an underserved lower tier where a 50-500 USD/month AI-driven subscription is credible? Evidence of small operators succeeding

### Takeaway
The price "hole" is real and documented on vendor pages: pharma patent/CI tools start at 8,000-24,000 USD/yr (DrugPatentWatch), FDA policy intelligence was launched at 25-75k USD/yr (AgencyIQ), Cortellis/Citeline are quote-only with third-party estimates of 15k-500k USD/yr, while tender alerts already exist at 25 EUR-342 USD/month — so the credible 50-500 USD/month space is (a) francophone/Africa/Morocco-specific pharma regulatory + tender intelligence and (b) narrow "one-question" monitors, not generic global tools. I found no verifiable revenue evidence of a solo operator in pharma regulatory/tender intelligence; the only proof is that low-priced small vendors exist and publish prices (Jorpex 49-149 USD/mo, Bibliovigilance from 119 USD/mo, Pharmatext 69 USD/user/mo).

### Inferences
- The 50-500 USD/month tier is most credible where incumbents are either quote-only global platforms (Cortellis, Citeline, Evaluate, Redica) or generic tender aggregators with no domain layer; it is least credible in EU/UK tender alerts, which are already commoditised at 25-150 USD/month.
- Best fit for "mostly AI-operated + low COI": #13 (cosmetics/supplements watch), #4/#5 (donor/TED health tender radar outside employer's bidding scope), #14 (devices), #19 (generic tender watch), #8 (inspection digest). Highest value but highest COI: #3, #6, #7, #10.
- Subscription "information" products carry far less liability and fewer vendor-qualification hurdles than GxP tools (#9, #11), which pharma QA will audit before buying; a solo vendor is realistic for newsletters/alerts (paid by card, no qualification) and unrealistic as a GxP-critical system supplier.

## Q2. Tender-monitoring markets (Morocco, francophone Africa, EU): size, prices, data availability and reuse terms

### Takeaway
EU tender data is legally the easiest raw material (TED is free, bulk/API, commercial reuse allowed with attribution), but EU alerting is commoditised (25 EUR to ~340 USD/month). Morocco is large (≈340 bn MAD public procurement in 2025; 54,000 firms on the portal) with a reported local watch price of 6,000-25,000 MAD/yr, but I could not verify the reuse terms of marchespublics.gov.ma or any Moroccan scraping case law — this is the main legal unknown.

### Inferences
- A Moroccan tender product should store notice metadata + links (not republish full documents), avoid personal data (names of signatories), and seek written confirmation or a data agreement from the TGR/portal operator; scraping legality is unverified, not established.
- Pricing headroom in Morocco: the 6-25k MAD/yr anchor (~50-200 USD/month) matches the target tier; a pharma-vertical version with award/price analytics could justify the upper end, but the buyer universe is small (~50-60 manufacturers + importers/distributors), so revenue ceiling is modest (inference).
- Francophone sub-Saharan tender information is thin and low-priced; donor-funded health procurement (Global Fund, UNICEF, World Bank) is a better target than national portals.

### Gaps
- Terms of use of marchespublics.gov.ma, UNGM, World Bank, AfDB, Global Fund and UNICEF procurement notices were not retrieved; no Moroccan case law on scraping found (one AI-law tracker rated Morocco "moderate risk" but with a doubtful legal citation — [ungovr](https://law.ungovr.org/ai/ma), not relied upon).
- No named Moroccan tender-watch vendor with a public price list was found; no market-size estimate for tender-alert services in Morocco or francophone Africa exists in the sources.
- A Togolese alert service priced around 10,000 FCFA/month appeared in a search summary without a verifiable URL — not confirmed.
- Total number of tenders published per year on the Moroccan portal (all procedures) not found.

## Q3. African/MENA pharma market data points indicating demand for regulatory/market intelligence

### Takeaway
Morocco is a mid-size, reform-heavy market (private market ≈10.4 bn MAD over 9 months of 2025; industry turnover 21-23 bn MAD; 50-60 industrial sites; 14,134 pharmacies) with a brand-new regulator (AMMPS) generating continuous rule changes, and Africa remains 70%+ import-dependent with partial continental harmonisation (AMA treaty ratified by ~29-31 of 55 states) — conditions that structurally create demand for regulatory watch, but the number of paying organisations in Morocco itself is small.

### Cited Findings

## Q5. Employment-contract / conflict-of-interest issues for a pharma employee running a pharma-related side business (Morocco)

### Takeaway
Moroccan sources agree on general principles — a duty of loyalty during the contract, enforceability of a contractual exclusivity clause if present, non-compete clauses governed by case law rather than the Labour Code, and disclosure of professional/trade secrets as a listed serious fault (art. 39, law 65-99) — so the decisive documents are the employee's own contract, the employer's code of conduct/outside-activities policy, and written employer consent; I could not retrieve the statutory texts themselves, and this is not legal advice.

### Cited Findings
- An employee wanting a side business must first check for an exclusivity clause and/or non-compete clause; the employee "is bound by a principle of loyalty towards the employer"; an exclusivity clause bars any other professional activity for the whole contract; the employer can authorise the activity by written agreement; the side business has its own tax/CNSS/VAT obligations (article 2014, updated Dec 2022) — [La Vie Éco](https://www.lavieeco.com/?p=56484)
- The non-compete clause is not provided for in the Moroccan Labour Code; it comes from practice and case law; in practice Moroccan employers rarely sue and judges rarely rule for the employer — [ReKrute](https://www.rekrute.com/emplois-clause-de-non-concurrence-un-vide-juridique-a-combler-conseils-carriere-759.html), [ReKrute](https://www.rekrute.com/fr/emplois-la-clause-de-non-concurrence-dans-votre-contrat-de-travail-conseils-carriere-1815.html)
- A Moroccan Cour de cassation ruling of 10 July 2002 is cited as setting cumulative validity conditions for non-compete clauses (details not verified) — [Village de la Justice](https://www.village-justice.com/articles/clause-non-concurrence-dans-contrat-cession-des-parts-sociales-liberte,39038.html)
- Art. 39 of the Labour Code gives an indicative list of serious faults (theft, breach of trust, unjustified absence, etc.); disclosure of professional secrets is cited among them; dismissal still requires the hearing procedure of arts. 62-65 — [CMS Francis Lefebvre Maroc](https://cms.law/en/mar/legal-updates/ce-qu-il-faut-savoir-sur-le-licenciement-pour-faute-grave-au-maroc), [ClicPaie guide (2026)](https://clicpaie.ma/blogs/code-du-travail-maroc/)
- Full text of law 65-99 is available for verification — [Upsilon Consulting copy](https://upsilon-consulting.com/code-travail-maroc-loi-65-99/)

### Inferences (general principles and mitigation — to be validated by a Moroccan employment lawyer)
- Risk ranks by overlap with the employer: (1) same customers/tenders/products (#3, #6, #7, #10) = competing activity and loyalty breach risk even without a clause; (2) same sector but non-overlapping segment (devices, cosmetics, supplements, donor tenders in countries where the employer is absent) = manageable with disclosure; (3) non-pharma (#19, #20 outside employer's suppliers) = lowest.
- Multinational pharma employers usually add a code of conduct requiring prior declaration/approval of outside activities and banning use of company time, equipment, name, and non-public information; breach is a disciplinary matter independent of labour-law clauses (general industry practice; not sourced for a specific employer).
- Practical mitigations: obtain written authorisation (the La Vie Éco source explicitly recommends a written agreement); build exclusively from public sources with a documented source log per data point; exclude employer's products, suppliers, tenders and customers from coverage; no work on employer devices/hours; operate through a separate legal entity with its own name; never sell to direct competitors services that draw on employer-specific know-how; keep the "regulated act" boundary (no pharmacist-responsible, QPPV, medical or legal advice roles).
- Confidentiality is the hard line: R&D pipeline, supplier prices/audits, tender pricing and BD targets learned at work cannot be distinguished by a client from "expert insight", so the safest positioning is data-product (traceable public data) rather than opinion-product.

### Gaps
- Exact Labour Code / DOC articles on exclusivity, moonlighting and loyalty were not retrieved; no Moroccan source found on whether an employee may hold auto-entrepreneur status alongside salaried employment or on limits tied to that status.
- No case law found on dismissal for an undeclared side business in Morocco; the 2002 Cour de cassation conditions were not read first-hand.
- Sector-specific rules (law 17-04 on who may run pharmaceutical establishments/activities; whether an information/consulting company needs any health authorisation) not researched.

## Annex — Public data sources and reuse terms (verified vs unverified)

### Takeaway
US and EU institutional data are the safest legal foundation (openFDA CC0; EMA and TED reusable commercially with attribution); Moroccan, African and UN/donor sources are usable for monitoring but their redistribution terms were not verified.

### Cited Findings
- openFDA: public domain / CC0 1.0 unless marked; commercial use allowed; exceptions for third-party-copyright data and GMDN content (licence needed for commercial services/AI training); access limits may apply; no warranty of accuracy — [openFDA terms](https://open.fda.gov/terms/)
- EMA website content: may be reproduced/distributed for commercial and non-commercial purposes provided EMA is acknowledged as source; not applicable to third-party content/photos — [EMA legal notice](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/about-us/legal-notice)
- TED: reuse under Decision 2011/833/EU, bulk XML + APIs + SPARQL, free — [TED data reuse](https://ted.europa.eu/en/help/data-reuse)
- ClinicalTrials.gov (classic terms page, may be outdated): data free to all; credit the source, keep data current, show processing date; some records may carry third-party copyright outside the US — [ClinicalTrials.gov terms](https://classic.clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/about-site/terms-conditions)
- FDA 483s obtainable via FOIA — [Redica](https://redica.com/who-can-see-form-fda-483s-and-where-do-i-get-them/)

### Inferences
- A product built on openFDA + Orange Book + EMA + TED + ClinicalTrials.gov is legally clean; value-add must come from linking these to non-machine-readable African/Moroccan sources, which is exactly where the legal terms are least clear.

### Gaps
- Terms not verified: EudraGMDP, EDQM CEP database, WHO Prequalification lists, FDA Orange Book/DMF list specifics, UNGM, World Bank, AfDB, Global Fund (Price & Quality Reporting), UNICEF Supply Division, AMMPS portal, Moroccan Bulletin Officiel, Office des Changes. Each needs a ToS read before commercial redistribution.
